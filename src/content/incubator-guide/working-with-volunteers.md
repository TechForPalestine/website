Probably the biggest thing that can change your trajectory is building a team. It’s easy to think that when you need volunteers you can identify them: I need a mobile engineer to build the app, for example. However, we have found that simply adding dedicated and motivated team members can be extremely valuable, even if you don’t know what they will do at the start.

When you get a team member, try to delegate work to them. Take valuable, important work, and ask them to complete it. It might be faster to do it yourself — it almost certainly would be — but your goal is to add an extra lane to the highway of your productivity. Have a goal of adding 3-4 generalists who you can trust to complete important work to a good standard.

Once you have a few people working with you, you’ll find huge advantages. There will be someone to take on the new opportunity that just arrived. There will be people with context to help you out of indecision. There will be capacity for someone to take on managing a big product area that frees you up to do things only you can do. You can have them take care of one major product or growth area while you handle other things.

## How to work with volunteers

- The right way to think about your new teammates is that this is their hobby, and you can keep them because this is how they enjoy spending their lives. It is a social activity, like volunteering at a soup kitchen or joining their local non-profit board. Your volunteers will come back because they make friends here, and this is how they spend their time now. Remember to organize your team like that - treat people like friends and colleagues, get to know them. Ask them about their weekends or their pets or whatever it is people talk about.
- Set up a weekly meeting, perhaps 2-3 of them per week. In the early days, I met the ethicsvc team on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays for an hour. That said, people are busy so if 1 hr per week or two makes more sense, do that.
- Have a channel you use. You can use your public channel in the discord, but most don’t. I recommend to set up a Signal group (or Whatsapp or whatever your preferred medium is). For ethicsvc we use a signal group for example. Some projects use a separate Discord.
- Make it easy to add people — this means you need to make sure that you limit access to key information such as user data or the identities of partners until your volunteers have built up trust. That trust obviously isn’t there at the start, but you need to be able to easily add people despite that, so set up your permissions accordingly.

## How to Find a Volunteer

Looking for extra support for your project? Follow these steps to find the right volunteer:

1️⃣ **Assess Your Needs-** determine what help you require and if needed, consult the T4P Volunteering team (**@\_volunteering** in Discord or **volunteering@techforpalestine.org**)

- **Ongoing support?** Get skilled volunteers committed **3+ months** for regular help. Keep in mind most people in our community have 1-5 hours weekly.
- **Need specialized or short-term help??** Access **Subject Matter Experts** for one-off tasks (e.g., professional consolations, user-testing, website design updates, data entry).
  - Consider leveraging Mighty Missions and the Mighty Missions community for one-off needs like user-testing.

2️⃣ **Define the Role**

- Outline clear tasks & responsibilities. Break down tasks and responsibilities into a clear, engaging job description.
- Consider if you should submit a Mighty Mission Request to [volunteering@techforpalestine.org](mailto:volunteering@techforpalestine.org)or mighty mission area of Mattermost vs publishing a new mighty mission to the hub for one-off short-term support like user-testing.

To determine if a Mighty Mission is best consider the following:

- A Mission should be **self-contained**, meaning ideally it can be completed without requiring any additional input or clarification once it’s published. The goal is for every community member to have everything they need to act immediately and confidently.

1. **A well-defined Mighty Mission should:** State the Mission in one clear sentence that specifies exactly what should be done and what you hope to achieve.
2. **Included estimated time to complete**
3. **Include step by step instructions and resources:** Provide everything needed for community members to complete it successfully, including context, necessary links or files, who to contact for feedback, and any resource you think would be helpful.
4. **Include any special considerations:** Any specific requirements like the number of testers you're targeting, release dates for particular functions, or other important notes.

3️⃣ **Clarify Requirements**

- Specify **must-have skills** vs. nice-to-haves (required: basic design skills, nice to have: growth marketing etc.)
- Open to beginners? Enthusiastic learners can be a great fit!

4️⃣ **Submit Your Request**

- The HUB – Select Team – Request a Volunteer
- For Mighty Missions, submit a mighty mission in the hub

5️⃣ **We’ll Find Your Match**

- Promote your role on [**T4P’s page**](https://techforpalestine.org/volunteer#open-roles) & LinkedIn.
- Our team will search our database for candidates. We will vet your basic requirements.

6️⃣ **Review & Respond**

- Schedule an interview with matched candidates **within 2 days**.
- Feel free to rely on our screening guidelines [here.](#volunteer-screening-guide-for-project-leaders)
- Provide feedback—even if it’s not a match. **Always respond to the candidate** and the Volunteering Team. If the fit isn’t right, encourage them to explore other opportunities through Volunteering Coordinator.

7️⃣ **Onboard & Welcome!**

- Use our templates to set them up for success.

Need help at any step? Reach out to the Volunteering Team—we’re happy to assist!

Contact: `volunteering@techforpalestine.org`

## Leader’s Best Practices for Engaging Volunteers

### 📝 Recruiting: Set the Foundation

- **Reply fast!** Quick responses show you’re engaged—and stop the ghosting before it starts. Aim to reply the same day or next.
- The Volunteering Team checks everyone’s availability, but **a quick double-check doesn’t hurt.** Before assigning tasks, make sure folks still have time to commit. Fewer drop-offs later!
- **Be real about the work and its impact** to attract the right type of volunteers.
- **Don’t downplay your expectations**. If it’s not a match, that’s okay! Offer kind feedback and guide them back to the Volunteering team. We will find another role where they can make the greatest impact.

**Pro Tip:** Have a voice call with each candidate before finalizing their role. A quick chat builds rapport and ensures alignment! Give a candidate a day to decide if they want to join.

### 🌟 Onboarding: Lay the Groundwork for Impact

- Welcome volunteers with a **friendly onboarding in the first few days**—live intros, quick guides, short videos, team meetings. It helps them feel like part of something bigger (because they are!)
- Busy? **Tag in a trusted teammate** to help out. It’s a great way to build community and leadership.
- Teach the tools! Not everyone is a pro—give them a quick review of tools they need to succeed in your project.
- Have volunteers confirm their weekly availability — what days/times will they set aside for their volunteer work and meeting availability as applicable.

**Pro Tip:** Create a ‘Week 1 Checklist’ so new volunteers know exactly what to do and where to start. Schedule the first task immediately. Clarity prevents hesitation and momentum is key to long-term commitment.

### 🔥 Making Volunteering Really Meaningful

- Walk members through the role, answer any questions, and set clear short-term goals to help them get going fast.
- **Get them involved immediately.** Waiting = disengagement.
- **Give every volunteer something to own,** no matter how small—responsibility fuels commitment.

**Pro Tip:** **Host showcases and share wins**—hearing from you or other projects inspires action! Knowing about the project’s real-world impact keeps morale high.

### 💬 Engagement: Keep Communication Clear & Open

- **Talk often!** Good communication is the glue that holds teams together (and stops them from falling into confusion). Chat regularly to boost engagement, retention, and satisfaction. Think of it as a process for long term relationship building.
- **Set a rhythm—daily or weekly check-ins** via your team's selected channels. It keeps everyone in the loop and avoids those “Wait… what are we doing again?” moments.
- Offer support and guidance, but **keep things flexible—** scheduling is the #1 concern for many new folks.
- **Lead by example!** Share your own schedule and availability—if you're off the grid, say so. Request your team to do the same especially for extended periods of time. Transparency builds trust, and trust builds awesome projects. Explain to team members the impact of their absence.

**Pro Tip:** Use casual check-ins (“Hey, how’s it going?”) to keep conversation flowing. A simple message can re-engage someone who’s drifting away!

### 🗣 Listen, Learn, and Improve:

- Projects that gather feedback retain volunteers better. Check-ins, meetings, and simple surveys can help keep the pulse on everyone’s experience.
- Actually act on feedback—**volunteers need to see their voices matter.**

**Pro Tip:** Run a quick “What’s working/What’s not?” session every 3 weeks to identify small tweaks that can make a big difference!

**Need more help?** Book a 1:1 with any T4P staff or other project leads. We’re all here to learn from each other—and to keep building for Palestine.✊

## Volunteer Screening Guide for Project Leaders

### First, Tailored Screening Methods

- **Choose the right format**: Video/voice calls for sensitive/long-term roles; for one off tasks async chats may suffice.

- **Understand and respect our privacy policy found here** [Volunteer Privacy at Tech for Palestine](#volunteer-privacy-at-tech-for-palestine).

  To protect T4P and the safety of all our community members, volunteers are required to share their real identity with select T4P members to be considered for open volunteer positions.

  Your real identity will only be shared with T4P members that need to know you to make informed volunteer placement decisions. This includes the Volunteer Coordination team, the hiring manager, and the T4P Executive team. Your identity will not be shared with others, and you may do all your work anonymously.

- When **setting up an interview, always ask:** _“Do you have any preferences or limitations for how we connect?”_ Offer alternatives: Voice only calls, text-based interviews, or initials-only profiles are okay when security is a concern.

- **Adjust time**: for example, 30 mins call for critical roles; 15 mins for simple tasks. Hiring in bulk for the same role — consider doing group information sessions. Use [Calendly](https://calendly.com/login) to let volunteers pick their ideal time to connect with you - no scheduling headaches!

### Approach Screening with Structure

1. **Communicate Mission & Expectations**
   - Highlight the project’s goals, time commitments, emotional demands, and team culture upfront.
   - Don’t downplay what your project needs!
   - Allow questions.
2. **Ask Purpose-Driven Questions (some examples below):**

- _“What motivates you to support this cause?”_
- _'’Could you share a reference or example of past similar work?”_
- _'’Have you worked as [a volunteer/ Social Media Manager/ Web Developer] before? If so, what did you learn?”_
- _'’Do you have experience with [Insert: Technology/Platform]? Is so, in what context”_
- _“Are you comfortable with [X time commitment/remote work/sensitive content]?”_
- _“How do you approach working in emotionally charged or politicized contexts?”_
- _“What support would help you succeed in this role?'’_

### How to Make a Decision?

**Clarify Must-Haves**

- Skills: Does their experience match core needs (e.g., fundraising, Arabic translation)? If not, do we have time and resources to support their learning?
- Values: Do they align with your specific project’s mission?

**Prioritize Passion Over Perfection**

- Beginners with dedication can grow into roles. Balance expertise with enthusiasm!

**Trust but Verify**

- For very sensitive positions, you may consider requesting Resume, LinkedIn Profile and/or references after initial screening.

### Final Step: Reconfirm Commitment

Ideally in writing. Example message: “_Please take 24-48 hours to reflect on whether you can commit to this role. We welcome any final questions you may have. If this still feels like the right fit, we'd love you to confirm your participation by [date].“_

**Pro Tip:** If unsure, involve the Volunteering Team—we’re here to help!

## Volunteer Privacy at Tech for Palestine

### Our Commitment

We understand some supporters wish to protect their privacy when contributing to the Palestinian cause. We support:

- Working under pseudonyms in public-facing activities
- Protecting your personal information
- Never attributing your work publicly without consent

### Internal Identification

For community security and integrity:

- Volunteers must provide their real identity to the T4P Core Management Team, Volunteer Coordination Team, and relevant Project Leaders when interviewing for open volunteer positions. You are not required to provide this information when submitting your volunteer submission form or at any time prior to interviewing for a specific role.
- For the safety of T4P and all our members, certain positions may require a formal ID verification through our trusted 3rd party identity verification platform. Your information will not be stored and will be deleted once the verification is complete.
- Your information and identity will not be shared with anyone else without your permission.

### Protection Measures

- Strict access controls limited to designated leadership
- No sharing with external organizations without your consent
- Secure communication protocols

Your contribution is valuable, and we honor your privacy needs while maintaining community safety. Questions? Or to be removed from our volunteer database. Contact: volunteering@techforpalestine.org.
