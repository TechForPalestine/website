## End to end

For some projects, especially processes, it’s useful to validate it end to end before you commit to it. For example, with Apricot (hiring Palestinians in Gaza remotely), we pushed them to get 5 people hired before they did anything else. This made sure they understood every part of the problem (pitching, interviews, payments, internet access) that they were solving.

This isn’t applicable for all projects, but for ones with a well-defined scope then making sure you truly do understand the components of that scope can be very valuable.

## B2B or B2C

Are you building for organizers, or protestors? For social media warriors, or specific journalists? Knowing which one you’re building is going to dramatically change how you build your product, what it does, how your go-to-market works, etc.

## Theory of change

A Theory of Change is an explanation for how you think your project brings change to the movement. Articulating how specifically you think the change occurs will significantly help you with product and market decisions.

At any point, you should be able to say “we believe that X is very important. As such, our project Y will have the effect Z, which will help push X in the right direction”.
