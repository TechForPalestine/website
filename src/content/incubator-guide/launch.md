Projects in the incubator usually follow the same path towards a launch: our first big push to thousands of people, through partners, our mailing list, our blog, our socials, influencers and friends who share our content with the community.

At first you won't be ready for a big launch. You wouldn't be able to make the most of it, and you'd waste the opportunity. A good launch needs a product that works, gives people value, keeps its users and, ideally, can grow on its own. Put simply, when people see your launch and try your product, you want them to think "this is great".

## The path to launch

**Find your first users.** Early on, you want a small number of users to validate your product. That might be 3, 10 or 100, depending on what you're building. Find them with a soft launch: a quick post on your socials asking for help. You're not pitching the idea, you're asking people to help you with it. For example: "We're building an AI assistant for activists. Can you help us put it through its paces?" Be humble and approachable. If you don't have enough reach, we can post on T4P's socials.

**Then find more.** Once you've validated what you're building, aim for perhaps ten times as many users. This still isn't a big launch. Ask friends and your networks to try it, or work with us to share it on T4P's channels.

**Get feedback at every stage.** Feedback is the reason to get users. Without it you can't iterate and you can't tell whether it's working. Set up ways to hear from people: a form on your site, a button in your app, error tracking, an email address for users, and actually emailing users with questions.

**Plan the big launch.** After several rounds, you may be ready. You'll know because exciting partners are lined up: people who heard about you on their own, were introduced by an enthusiastic activist, or met you through T4P (we often mention projects to partners we think will be interested). A couple of weeks before you're ready, tell our marketing team and we'll plan the launch with you.

## Get your product ready

When you launch, you want to make the most of the attention. Don't go overboard, but take some basic steps so that:

- people have a good experience and everything works;
- you can keep in touch with the people who sign up;
- people who want to get involved can; and
- people have a reason to share your project.

### Make sure everything works

- Run through your sign-up and onboarding yourself every day.
- Test by hand on phones and computers, at different screen sizes and in different languages.
- Add error tracking so you know when something breaks.

### Keep the people who come

Most people forget a site soon after trying it, even if they liked it. Give them a reason to come back. Collecting an email address is the basic step, and better than nothing. Often you can do better: let people follow something in your app and get notified about it, such as newly boycotted products, new zines, or whatever they came to your site for.

### Make it easy to share

Give people a good reason to share your project, and make sharing easy. If your app lists protests, let people send a protest to a friend so they might go too. If you encourage people to boycott, let them show their friends how much they've boycotted, with an image or link they can post in the group chat.

## Think about growth

Ask what your product itself can do to help it grow. One way to think about your users' journey is the AARRR framework:

- **Acquisition:** getting people to your site.
- **Activation:** getting them to sign up.
- **Revenue:** getting them to take the action that matters for your project. For most projects this isn't payment.
- **Retention:** making sure they come back.
- **Referral:** getting them to bring others.

Before you launch, go through each stage and rate yourself honestly. Can people quickly understand what your app does and why they should sign up? Does sign-up take only a few steps, with nothing unnecessary? Will people remember to come back if they found it useful? Can they use it with friends?

Later, this framework will guide how you grow. Before launch, it's enough to understand where you stand and pick up any easy wins. Don't delay your launch to make it perfect. Fix the obvious problems; there'll be plenty of chances to improve after launch.
