## Path to launch

New members of T4P typically have the same trajectory through the incubator, working towards the goal of having a “launch”. A launch is our first big push to thousands of people, using partners, our mailing list, our blog, our range of socials, influencers, and other friends who promote our content to the community.

However, initially you will not be ready for a big launch, because you will not be able to take advantage of it, and you will waste the opportunity. In order to be able to have a “good launch”, you need a product which works, provides value, which can retain users, and potentially which can grow naturally. Simply speaking, when you launch, you want users to see it, use it, and think “this is great”.

### Process to get there

Early on, you want to get early users to validate your product. This could possibly be 3 users, 10 users, or 100 users depending on the product. You can get these users by “soft launching” - just post a quick thing on your socials asking for help. You’re not pitching them on the idea, you’re asking for their help with it. If you don’t have enough reach, we can help by posting on T4P socials. An example here might be “I’m looking for help testing out the new AI we’re building for activists at pal-chat. Can you help us put our ChatGPT-for-Palestine through its paces?”. Generally, be humble and approachable at this stage.

After you have validated what you’re building, then you want maybe 10x more users, again not a big launch. Again, humbly ask favors from your friends and socials to try out your product, or work with T4P to get the content on ours.

It’s important at each of these stages to get feedback - that is the purpose of getting users. If you’re not getting feedback, you can’t iterate, you can’t tell if it’s working. So make sure you have feedback mechanisms - perhaps a form on the website, a button in the app, a bug tracker like Sentry, an email address for your users (where you actually email them and ask them questions), or all of the above.

Once you’ve gone through multiple rounds you may be ready for a “big launch”. You’ll know you’re ready because you already have a bunch of exciting partners lined up, who will have heard about you organically, or who you will have been introduced to by an excited activist (or by T4P, as we often mention products to partners if we think it will interest them).

When you feel you’re nearly ready (a couple of weeks before being ready), reach out to @\_marketing in your private Discord channel, and we’ll work with you on the launch.

### Preparing product for launch

When you’re getting ready to launch, you want to make the most of it, ensuring that you get the most out of the traffic that comes your way. Obviously you don’t want to go overboard, but we recommend taking some basic steps that ensure:

- Users have a good experience, and that everything works.
- You can capture users and contact them again
- You can get some users involved if they want to
- Users are incentivized to share with others where possible

**Ensuring everything works**

- Do daily runthroughs of your user onboarding flow
  - Ensure you’ve manually tested on mobile and desktop, preferably at different resolutions, languages, etc
- Add error tracking to ensure you will know if things break all the time and users have a bad experience

**Capture the user**  
You want to make sure that users who sign up stay in some meaningful way. Most people when they try a site, they immediately forget about it, even if they thought it was cool. You want some way to remind them to come back

Collecting an email is a very basic move that’s better than nothing, though you can often do better. Allowing them to “follow” something in your app or site might be a good approach. Get notified where there are new boycotted products, or new zines, whatever it is the user came to your site to do.

**Spread the love**  
If you can, find a good reason for someone to share your app with friends, and make that as easy as possible. For example, if your app lists protests, make sure people can send a link to that protest to a friend so that they might go too. If you’re encouraging the user to boycott, make sure they can show off how much they’ve boycotted to their friends in the group chat (that is, your app should have an image or a link to show off their wins.

## Thinking about growth

As part of building your app, you want to think about “product-led growth”: what can I do with my product to encourage its growth.

You can often split the different phases of your user journey using the “AARRR” framework:

- **A**cquisition - getting a user to the site
- **A**ctivation - getting a user signed up
- **R**evenue - getting a user to pay (or whatever the “value” action is for your tool or site)
- **R**etention - ensuring the user comes back
- **R**eferral - getting the user to bring other users onto the platform

Before launching, think through each aspect of your product and rate yourself on how you perform. Is the user easily able to understand what the app does and why to sign up? Is the sign up optimized so that it only takes a few minutes or a few steps, with no superfluous steps? Is it easy for the user to remember to come back if they find value? Can they use it with friends?

Later, this AARRR framework will form the basis of how you grow and scale your impact. However, before launch, just going through each part and understanding where you are – and perhaps if there are easy wins to be had–will be enough.

You don’t want to delay the launch to get this perfect, just some quick fixes and ensuring nothing is terrible. After launch, there will be plenty more opportunities to optimize it and get more users onboard.
