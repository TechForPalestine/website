## How to give an update

At Office Hours you'll be asked for an update on how things are going, and we ask for a written update each month. Here's how to make them useful.

An update should be about your progress towards your goals. Specific actions, features and team changes aren't very helpful unless they relate to a goal or to a question you have.

### A weak update

> We added a new feature to sort the listings. Our design needed some polish and we're working on that. We also added a new team member on community. So that's what we've been up to. Oh, can you introduce us to some journalists?

### A strong update

> We launched recently and had really good feedback, so our top priority is growth off the back of that. We have 6,000 users and we're growing 6% a week. We want to reach 10% this month. Our main problem is that only about 3% of new visitors sign up, and we think we can improve that a lot.
>
> Feedback from people who left tells us the design confuses them, and that they expected to be able to save listings. That's on our homepage and our roadmap but isn't out yet. We're about to release a design fix based on a user study we just finished, and saving is being tested with a few users now. We think this will double our sign-up rate, and we'll know within two weeks.
>
> We also found that users don't know about our community forum, which could really help them. We've neglected it for lack of capacity, so we've just brought on a volunteer to run it.
>
> Question: if sign-ups go above 5%, we think we'll be in a good position for media attention. Do you know any journalists who cover this area?

### Why the second is better

It starts with your key goals right now and ties them to your overall goals. Then it ties each piece of news to those goals, and ends with a specific question. The same format is useful for your own notes, and for keeping your team aligned on what you've achieved.

## Keeping up motivation

The fate of Palestine does not rest on your shoulders. You are one part of a big machine, and that machine is part of a much bigger one. You can't take responsibility for every terrible thing that happens. Your place in the movement is making your project succeed.

When you see an atrocity, the best way to respond is often to get back to work. You can't solve it directly. You can only do your work. With an army, a billion dollars or a government, you might respond differently. You don't have those; you're responding with your project.

When something needs to be called out, of course, share it. Support your allies and colleagues. But don't take on the guilt of not having solved a problem that started before you were born.

It helps to spend less time looking at the genocide. We're told "don't look away", and bearing witness has value. But you can't help Palestine if you're overwhelmed. You may need some distance, as hard as that is to say. Consider a break from social media. Focus on the good you can do with your project. Even if it doesn't help today, this is how we make change.

Palestine needs you to keep going for the long term.
