<p>In this section, we assess the various influence attacks on Wikipedia coming from Israel and the Israel Lobby.</p>
<p>These attacks attempt to influence Wikipedia, leverage the power of AIPAC-aligned U.S. members of Congress and other government officials.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-1">Section 4.1</a>, we show that twenty-three AIPAC-funded U.S. members of Congress, led by Debbie Wasserman Schultz, leveraged the ADL report to put pressure on Wikimedia to identify editors, push back against anti-Zionism, and to push Wikipedia to use AI-based tools to automatically enforce policy, inserting an arbiter of truth which would presumably override Wikipedia editors.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-2">Section 4.2</a>, we show that Ed Martin, then Interim U.S. Attorney for Washington D.C., leveraged information provided in the ADL report to attack Wikimedia’s 501(c)(3) designation, which allows it to fundraise for charitable donations in the U.S. Martin’s attack sought to create a means of influence over Wikipedia editors, potentially setting up grounds to remove anonymity, influence Wikipedia’s content moderation processes, and remove editors on the basis of their beliefs.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-3">Section 4.3</a>, we document an additional attack launched by two AIPAC-funded Chairs of U.S. Congressional Committees, James Comer, Chair of Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, and Nancy Mace, Chair of the Subcommittee on Cybersecurity, Information Technology, and Government Innovation. These attacks asked for identifying information about Wikipedia editors, and made claims about bias, antisemitism, coordination by nation-state actors, and coordination within academia.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-4">Section 4.4</a>, we document another attack launched by AIPAC-funded Senator Ted Cruz, acting in the capacity as Chair of the U.S. Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation. Cruz’s letter cited the ADL’s claims indirectly through references to Wasserman Schultz’s letter, repeated the claim of a “coordinated group of editors” who “pushed antisemitic narratives” on Wikipedia, and demanded a broad set of documents, including information on ArbCom cases.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-5">Section 4.5</a>, we discuss the surveillance operation on Wikipedia editors, outlining how multiple organizations are surveilling Wikipedia editors, including the Heritage Foundation, the ADL, a private Israeli-led company led by one of the instigators of the attacks, and Builders for Tomorrow, the “stealth nonprofit startup” identified as a partner by the ADL. We also discuss which U.S. and European laws these organizations may be breaking, according to their own documentation.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-6">Section 4.6</a>, we review the recent appointment of Wikimedia’s new CEO, and her past as a U.S. diplomat, and in particular her close collaboration with pro-Israel organizations as U.S. Ambassador to Chile. We question whether her appointment is a result of the influence attack, which occurred during Wikimedia’s search for a CEO.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-7">Section 4.7</a>, we continue to show the extent to which pro-Israel individuals and groups seek to spread their influence through not only Hasbara propaganda disseminated online, but also through manipulating institutions. In this section, we discuss how Israeli manipulations also attack institutions of higher learning and academia, as an extension from their attacks on academic research, that which is cited on platforms like Wikipedia, and that supports and fuels organizing efforts of the Palestine movement. We share various examples of Israel’s attacks on institutions of higher learning and academia, specifically universities in the U.S., attacks on the 2024 university encampments, widespread efforts to silence pro-Palestine voices coming from universities, such as through firing and/or doxing professors and students, as well as efforts to shut down academic events, courses, or even full departments.</p>

<p>They demanded that Wikimedia answer a series of questions, questions that clearly aim to force Wikipedia to accept pro-Israel influence from the ADL and other organizations. For example, in the letter they ask “What steps are being taken to ensure sufficient oversight of editors and administrators to prevent biased or coordinated manipulation of content?”, and “Has the Wikimedia Foundation investigated if prolific Wikipedia editors and administrators are covertly acting on behalf of adversaries of the United States, including Iran, Hamas, and other antagonistic foreign entities?”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-148" id="fnref-148">148</a></sup></p>

<h2 id="s4-2">4.2 U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia</h2>
<p>The letter sent to Wikimedia by Ed Martin, short-term interim United States Attorney for the District of Columbia, attacks Wikipedia from a third angle—its charitable 501(c)(3) tax exempt status.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-151" id="fnref-151">151</a></sup></p>
<p>Like the letter from Congress, we see Martin pushing for editorial oversight and content moderation, and he specifically references Trust and Safety. This is one of the mechanisms frequently used by Israel to limit the spread of negative content on Israel, or any content supportive of Palestinian liberation.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-152" id="fnref-152">152</a></sup></p>
<p>Requiring Wikipedia to add editorial oversight and content moderation would provide pro-Israel groups with the opportunity to moderate the platform into taking an explicitly pro-Israel stance, a clear goal of these efforts. This oversight would likely be provided by the ADL, an openly pro-Israel organization that has, for over a decade, pushed to partner with tech firms to censor and surveil online content, and whose deep ideological bias makes it fundamentally inappropriate to serve as an objective moderator for these companies.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-153" id="fnref-153">153</a></sup> Despite this conflict of interest, the ADL holds great corporate influence; according to ADL CEO Jonathan Greenblatt, the ADL already works with “every company from Alphabet to Zoom”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-154" id="fnref-154">154</a></sup></p>
<p>The Israel Lobby seeks to have this same deep influence over Wikipedia. Despite having an extremely robust content moderation system, which has matured over twenty years, Martin’s April 2025 letter shows an attempt to create space for a biased, pro-Israel moderation system to be added. This would, in effect, require editors to submit their fact-checking to the Israel Lobby for approval.</p>
<p>Legal observers also noted procedural irregularities with Martin’s letter: the letter incorrectly stated that Wikimedia was incorporated in Washington D.C. rather than Florida, and investigations into tax-exempt status are typically handled by the IRS rather than federal prosecutors.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-155" id="fnref-155">155</a></sup> Wikipedia editor Molly White characterized the letter as “the Trump administration weaponizing laws to try to silence high-quality independent information”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-156" id="fnref-156">156</a></sup></p>

<h3 id="s4-5-1">4.5.1 The Heritage Foundation</h3>
<p>The Heritage Foundation, a prominent conservative think tank based in Washington D.C., has been influencing U.S. policy on various topics since 1973, including welfare, education, the economy, crime, and foreign policy.</p>
<p>In 2023, and in anticipation of Trump’s second term, the Heritage Foundation received increased attention for the publication of “Project 2025”, an initiative aimed at consolidating power on the executive level in favor of more right-leaning policies.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-165" id="fnref-165">165</a></sup> Some notable features of Project 2025 include advocating for the dismantling of the Department of Education, Medicare, and DEI programs (Diversity, Equity and Inclusion), while also supporting the mass detention and deportation of immigrants, and the curtailing of women’s reproductive rights.</p>
<p>Observers have indicated that about 53% of Project 2025 policies have already been put into place only a year into Trump’s term.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-166" id="fnref-166">166</a></sup></p>
<h4 id="s4-5-1-1">4.5.1.1 Project Esther: Attacking Pro-Palestinian Activism</h4>
<p>The Heritage Foundation is staunchly pro-Israel, and has long held this stance. Just recently, and despite and amidst Israel’s genocide in Gaza, the organization advocated for the U.S. to upgrade its “special relationship” with Israel to a “strategic partnership”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-167" id="fnref-167">167</a></sup></p>
<p>In October 2024, the Heritage Foundation announced Project Esther,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-168" id="fnref-168">168</a></sup> which the New York Times described as “a plan to crush the pro-Palestinian movement”,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-169" id="fnref-169">169</a></sup> explaining that “Project Esther’s architects envisioned outcomes that at the time might have seemed far-fetched”, explaining that in the plan,</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“Curriculum it believed to be sympathetic to a ‘Hamas support’ narrative would be taken out of schools and universities, and ‘supporting faculty’ would be removed. Social media would be purged of content deemed to be antisemitic. Institutions would lose public funding. Foreign students who pushed for Palestinian rights would have their visas revoked, or be deported.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-170" id="fnref-170">170</a></sup></p>
</blockquote>
<p>Project Esther aims to silence any critique of Israel's actions in Gaza, and to associate the Palestinian solidarity movement with a so-called “Hamas Support Network”. In attempting to delegitimize the movement's claims by reducing its goals to the “support for Hamas”, Project Esther aims to disrupt various activist activities that are critical to movement-building, such as raising funds, meeting with elected officials, and partaking in visible advocacy campaigns on social media.</p>
<p>Project Esther has been criticized for weaponizing antisemitism to advocate for the Heritage Foundation’s far-right goals. According to the Jewish publication <em>The Forward</em>, the group “has struggled to attract Jewish supporters for its antisemitism plan, which appears to have been assembled by several evangelical Christian groups”, in particular because it doesn’t touch on right-wing antisemitism<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-171" id="fnref-171">171</a></sup>—rather it targets the pro-Palestine movement. <em>The Forward</em> discusses how internal documents show the Heritage Foundation making antisemitic claims, including suggesting that Jewish “masterminds” are behind the pro-Palestine movement.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-172" id="fnref-172">172</a></sup> <em>The Forward</em> also pointed out that “no major Jewish organizations appear to have participated in drafting the plan, or publicly endorsed it since its release”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-173" id="fnref-173">173</a></sup></p>
<p>Despite this, Project Esther has seen large uptake, with <em>The New York Times</em> reporting that “the White House and other Republicans have called for actions that appear to mirror more than half of Project Esther’s proposals, including threats to withhold billions in federal funding at universities and attempts to deport legal residents”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-174" id="fnref-174">174</a></sup></p>
<h4 id="s4-5-1-2">4.5.1.2 Wikipedia surveillance by Heritage Foundation</h4>
<p>In leaked documents<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-175" id="fnref-175">175</a></sup> published by <em>The Forward</em> in January 2025, it was revealed that the Heritage Foundation is targeting Wikipedia volunteers as part of its pro-Israel work in Project Esther, using advanced, and in many cases illegal, technical tools to dox Wikipedia authors.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-176" id="fnref-176">176</a></sup> Doxed authors would presumably receive the ire of the establishment, including potentially deportation and arrest. <p>In December 2025, two California universities made the news for targeting academics expressing solidarity with Palestinian rights. At University of California, Berkeley, computer science lecturer Peyrin Kao was suspended for six months without pay after sharing pro-Palestinian views in his classroom, including a brief discussion about technology ethics related to the Israeli military and mentioning his 38-day hunger strike for Gaza. The university cited violations of Regents Policy 2301 prohibiting “misuse of the classroom” and “political indoctrination” of students,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-230" id="fnref-230">230</a></sup> although Kao reiterated that he followed university policy as he shared his comments after class time had ended and had allowed students to leave. His suspension occurred a few months after UC Berkeley had shared his name with the Trump administration as part of a larger antisemitism investigation.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-231" id="fnref-231">231</a></sup></p>
<p>And at San Jose State University, university administration fired tenured Justice Studies Professor Sang Hea Kil. Kil was terminated for her pro-Palestinian advocacy, including her participation in campus protests and the student encampment. The university's president overrode the faculty-led review process, which had found that Kil had “already endured significant consequences” and expressed “concerning evidence” of external influence in the disciplinary process. CAIR-SFBA has condemned the dismissal as a violation of shared governance and academic freedom, noting that the administration discarded its own faculty committee's findings to impose the “harshest possible sanction”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-232" id="fnref-232">232</a></sup> Both of these recent cases demonstrate a disturbing pattern of targeting academics who speak out about Palestinian rights, creating an environment in which free speech and academic freedoms are suppressed, while selectively enforcing policies to target those advocating for Palestine.</p>
