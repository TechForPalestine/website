<p>In this section, we assess the various influence attacks on Wikipedia coming from Israel and the Israel Lobby.</p>
<p>These attacks attempt to influence Wikipedia, leverage the power of AIPAC-aligned U.S. members of Congress and other government officials. That influence over Wikipedia will allow it to transform the narrative on the site, which will feed back into AI.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-1">Section 4.1</a>, we show that twenty-three AIPAC-funded U.S. members of Congress, led by Debbie Wasserman Schultz, leveraged the ADL report to put pressure on Wikimedia to identify editors, push back against anti-Zionism, and to push Wikipedia to use AI-based tools to automatically enforce policy, inserting an arbiter of truth which would presumably override Wikipedia editors.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-2">Section 4.2</a>, we show that Ed Martin, then Interim U.S. Attorney for Washington D.C., leveraged information provided in the ADL report to attack Wikimedia’s 501(c)(3) designation, which allows it to fundraise for charitable donations in the U.S. Martin’s attack sought to create a means of influence over Wikipedia editors, potentially setting up grounds to remove anonymity, influence Wikipedia’s content moderation processes, and remove editors on the basis of their beliefs.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-3">Section 4.3</a>, we document an additional attack launched by two AIPAC-funded Chairs of U.S. Congressional Committees, James Comer, Chair of Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, and Nancy Mace, Chair of the Subcommittee on Cybersecurity, Information Technology, and Government Innovation. These attacks asked for identifying information about Wikipedia editors, and made claims about bias, antisemitism, coordination by nation-state actors, and coordination within academia.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-4">Section 4.4</a>, we document another attack launched by AIPAC-funded Senator Ted Cruz, acting in the capacity as Chair of the U.S. Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation. Cruz’s letter cited the ADL’s claims indirectly through references to Wasserman Schultz’s letter, repeated the claim of a “coordinated group of editors” who “pushed antisemitic narratives” on Wikipedia, and demanded a broad set of documents, including information on ArbCom cases.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-6">Section 4.6</a>, we discuss the surveillance operation on Wikipedia editors, outlining how multiple organizations are surveilling Wikipedia editors, including the Heritage Foundation, the ADL, a private Israeli-led company led by one of the instigators of the attacks, and Builders for Tomorrow, the “stealth nonprofit startup” identified as a partner by the ADL. We also discuss which U.S. and European laws these organizations may be breaking, according to their own documentation.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-7">Section 4.7</a>, we review the recent appointment of Wikimedia’s new CEO, and her past as a U.S. diplomat, and in particular her close collaboration with pro-Israel organizations as U.S. Ambassador to Chile. We question whether her appointment is a result of the influence attack, which occurred during Wikimedia’s search for a CEO.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-8">Section 4.8</a>, we connect the attacks against Wikipedia to the attacks on U.S. universities, which produce a significant amount of the scholarship that Wikipedia cites. By controlling what universities can research and what students and faculty can say, the Israel Lobby can limit the scholarly research they produce. This reduces scholarly work available to Wikipedia, allowing more space for spun narratives from biased media.</p>
<h2 id="s4-1">4.1 Wasserman Schultz and 22 other members of Congress</h2>
<p>Twenty-three members of Congress, from both parties and led by Debbie Wasserman Schultz, sent a letter to the Wikimedia foundation demanding answers to a series of questions.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-159" id="fnref-159">159</a></sup> In the letter, they reference the ADL report, and allege that “a group of approximately 30 Wikipedia editors who have significantly undermined the platform's credibility, making more than 1.5 million edits over the past decade to gradually and systematically distort neutral narratives on articles related to Israel, pushing an antisemitic, pro-Hamas, anti-Zionist, and anti-Israel agenda”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-160" id="fnref-160">160</a></sup></p>
<p>They demanded that Wikimedia answer a series of questions, questions that clearly aim to force Wikipedia to accept pro-Israel influence from the ADL and other organizations. For example, in the letter they ask “What steps are being taken to ensure sufficient oversight of editors and administrators to prevent biased or coordinated manipulation of content?”, and “Has the Wikimedia Foundation investigated if prolific Wikipedia editors and administrators are covertly acting on behalf of adversaries of the United States, including Iran, Hamas, and other antagonistic foreign entities?”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-161" id="fnref-161">161</a></sup></p>
<p>In doing so, they are setting the stage for state interference in Wikipedia, and a system of compliance, which will allow them to have significant influence over the content of the online encyclopedia. Their goal in exerting influence is explicitly stated as “to prevent and address antisemitic, anti-Zionist, and anti-Israel bias among its editors”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-162" id="fnref-162">162</a></sup></p>

<h2 id="s4-2">4.2 U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia</h2>
<p>The letter sent to Wikimedia by Ed Martin, short-term interim United States Attorney for the District of Columbia, attacks Wikipedia from a third angle—its charitable 501(c)(3) tax exempt status.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-164" id="fnref-164">164</a></sup></p>
<p>Like the letter from Congress, we see Martin pushing for editorial oversight and content moderation, and he specifically references Trust and Safety. This is one of the mechanisms frequently used by Israel to limit the spread of negative content on Israel, or any content supportive of Palestinian liberation.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-165" id="fnref-165">165</a></sup></p>
<p>Requiring Wikipedia to add editorial oversight and content moderation would provide pro-Israel groups with the opportunity to moderate the platform into taking an explicitly pro-Israel stance, a clear goal of these efforts. This oversight would likely be provided by the ADL, an openly pro-Israel organization that has, for over a decade, pushed to partner with tech firms to censor and surveil online content, and whose deep ideological bias makes it fundamentally inappropriate to serve as an objective moderator for these companies.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-166" id="fnref-166">166</a></sup> Despite this conflict of interest, the ADL holds great corporate influence; according to ADL CEO Jonathan Greenblatt, the ADL already works with “every company from Alphabet to Zoom”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-167" id="fnref-167">167</a></sup></p>
<p>The Israel Lobby seeks to have this same deep influence over Wikipedia. Despite having an extremely robust content moderation system, which has matured over twenty years, Martin’s April 2025 letter shows an attempt to create space for a biased, pro-Israel moderation system to be added. This would, in effect, require editors to submit their fact-checking to the Israel Lobby for approval.</p>
<p>Legal observers also noted procedural irregularities with Martin’s letter: the letter incorrectly stated that Wikimedia was incorporated in Washington D.C. rather than Florida, and investigations into tax-exempt status are typically handled by the IRS rather than federal prosecutors.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-168" id="fnref-168">168</a></sup> Wikipedia editor Molly White characterized the letter as the Trump administration “weaponizing laws to try to silence high-quality independent information”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-169" id="fnref-169">169</a></sup></p>

<h2 id="s4-5">4.5 The Attack on ArbCom</h2>

<p>ArbCom, Wikipedia’s arbitration committee which handles disputes between editors, has increasingly banned editors of Palestine-Israel content, banning at least 14 editors in 2025.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-178" id="fnref-178">178</a></sup> ArbCom is composed of editor-elected volunteers, and is tasked with policing misconduct. According to the agenda<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-248" id="fnref-248">248</a></sup> for the event, the participants signed the Santiago Declaration,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-249" id="fnref-249">249</a></sup> which pushed for the adoption of the IHRA definition and for support of Israel in the war in Gaza.</p>
<p>Finally, in December 2024, Meehan received the Maccabi Spirit award from the explicitly zionist organization CJCh, for “her defense of freedom and democracy”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-250" id="fnref-250">250</a></sup></p>
<p>It is fair to say that many of these meetings and events were part of her role as U.S. ambassador, and demonstrated U.S. policy rather than her own viewpoints. Being given an award from a zionist organization, and especially choosing to accept the award, crosses an already-tenuous line from U.S. representative to personal choice.</p>
<p>The purpose of the sort of attack launched by the ADL and the U.S. government is to gain influence over Wikipedia. A change of leadership to someone more sympathetic to their cause is the exact sort of outcome that influence seeks. A former career U.S. diplomat with a significant history of working with zionist organizations is a strong outcome for the ADL and AIPAC from their work.</p>
<p>In fact, in the second ADL report, “Damage Done”, the ADL says that it “welcomes the arrival of a new CEO at a critical moment for the platform”.</p>
<p>We discuss recommendations for the Wikimedia Foundation in regard to Meehan’s appointment in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-7#s7-1-7">Section 7.1.7</a>.</p>
<h2 id="s4-8">4.8 Attacks on Scholarship</h2>
<p>Because Wikipedia summarizes scholarly consensus, it draws heavily on research produced at U.S. universities. When the Israel Lobby attacks academic freedom and scholarship at these institutions, it undermines and directly threatens the foundational sources that inform Wikipedia.</p>
<p>In 2024, students at over 150 universities started Palestine solidarity encampments, pushing for an end to Israel’s genocide in Palestine.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-251" id="fnref-251">251</a></sup> The attacks on universities in 2025, under the pretense of “fighting antisemitism”, were actually attempts to reshape the landscape of U.S. universities.</p>
<p>A range of forces pushed back, including student lawsuits, the Trump and Biden administrations, university faculty members, trustees, and donors aiming to destroy any Palestinian solidarity across the university landscape. In response to these pressures and legal settlements, universities enacted a range of policy changes designed to stifle not only pro-Palestinian protests, but also academic speech and research.</p>
<p>These included changes to Middle East Studies departments, ending partnerships with Palestinian universities, demanding institutional policy changes, and firing professors deemed friendly to Palestine. They also targeted student free speech and ability to participate in protest or solidarity movements, potentially cutting off the supply of the next generation of scholars.</p>
<p>These attacks closely follow the Heritage Foundation’s Project Esther strategy to target students, scholars, and activists advocating for Palestinian liberation, as well as university funding.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-252" id="fnref-252">252</a></sup></p>
<h3 id="s4-8-1">4.8.1 Attacks on Middle East Studies</h3>
<p>The political pressure placed on universities had the direct goal of shutting down academic research related to Palestine.</p>
<p>In March 2025, the Trump administration canceled approximately $400 million in federal grants and contracts to Columbia University, “due to the school’s continued inaction in the face of persistent harassment of Jewish students,” or their alleged failure to adequately address antisemitism.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-253" id="fnref-253">253</a></sup></p>
<p>In February 2025, New York Governor Kathy Hochul ordered the City University of New York (CUNY) to remove a job posting for a professorship in Palestinian Studies at Hunter College. Governor Hochul claimed the words “settler colonialism”, “genocide”, and “apartheid” in the job posting “amounted to antisemitic attacks” and ordered CUNY to “immediately remove” the posting. CUNY complied a few hours later.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-254" id="fnref-254">254</a></sup></p>
<p>At Duke, the Persian language program was cut, the joint Duke–University of North Carolina Graduate Certificate in Middle East Studies was indefinitely suspended after spring 2025, and a required course was discontinued. These changes have made it more difficult for students to pursue language study and graduate-level research on the region.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-255" id="fnref-255">255</a></sup></p>
<p>In August 2026, whistleblower allegations revealed that federal officials withheld information from the investigations on antisemitism at Brown University, which showed that the allegations were unfounded. The complaint also described plans to question Middle East studies professors, particularly Muslim faculty, who were unrelated to the allegations under investigation.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-256" id="fnref-256">256</a></sup></p>
<p>In July 2023, Representative Elise Stefanik and 27 other Republican members of Congress sent a letter to Harvard leadership demanding that the university end its research partnership with Birzeit University in Palestine. In March 2025, following an internal review, Harvard’s School of Public Health suspended the partnership.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-257" id="fnref-257">257</a></sup></p>
<p>On March 28, 2025, Dean Marla Frederick announced the suspension of the Religion, Conflict, and Peace Initiative following a review of the Religion and Public Life program amid budget cuts and public accusations that its programming presented a “one-sided view of the Israel-Palestine conflict.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-258" id="fnref-258">258</a></sup></p>
<h3 id="s4-8-2">4.8.2 Policing What Can Be Researched</h3>
<p>One of the goals of the attacks on U.S. universities was to force them to incorporate the flawed IHRA definition of antisemitism, which we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-2#s2-3-2">Section 2.3.2</a>. This conflates critique of Israel and Zionism with antisemitism, which can then be weaponized against students and staff.</p>
<p>Under Columbia’s July 2025 agreement, most of the terminated or paused federal research grants in March 2025 were to be reinstated, but as part of the agreement, the university was to take on the IHRA definition as its official understanding of antisemitism.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-259" id="fnref-259">259</a></sup></p>
<p>Palestinian-American historian Rashid Khalidi cancelled his popular fall 2025 lecture course History of the Modern Middle East, citing Columbia’s adoption of the IHRA definition, which he says “deliberately, mendaciously and disingenuously conflates Jewishness with Israel, so that any criticism of Israel, or indeed description of Israeli policies, becomes a criticism of Jews”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-260" id="fnref-260">260</a></sup></p>
<p>In June 2025, the Harvard Education Publishing Group (HEPG), publisher of the Harvard Educational Review, cancelled a planned special issue on education and Palestine just weeks before publication. HEPG executive director Jessica Fiorillo communicated the decision to contributors after the publisher had sought legal review from Harvard’s Office of the General Counsel amid concerns that publication could prompt accusations of antisemitism. Contributors and journal editors challenged the cancellation, while the publisher defended its decision on editorial grounds.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-261" id="fnref-261">261</a></sup></p>
<p>Of course, universities should address and take action to combat discrimination, including antisemitism, in all forms. But the incorporation of the IHRA definition on antisemitism is instead weaponized to suppress academic freedoms, political speech, scholarly inquiry, critical thinking, and legitimate critique, all of which are supposed to be protected by academic institutions.</p>
<h3 id="s4-8-3">4.8.3 Targeting the Next Generation of Researchers</h3>
<p>At San José State University, university administration fired tenured Justice Studies Professor Sang Hea Kil. Kil was terminated for her pro-Palestinian advocacy, including her participation in campus protests and the student encampment.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-262" id="fnref-262">262</a></sup> In June 2026, an arbitrator ordered Kil’s reinstatement with full back pay, stating that the university had gone too far with its sanctions.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-263" id="fnref-263">263</a></sup></p>
<p>An October 2026 study documented 196 incidents of employer retaliation for Palestine-related speech in U.S. and Canadian higher education, including 76 terminations, and drew on interviews with 31 affected employees.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-264" id="fnref-264">264</a></sup></p>
<p>In early 2025, two high-profile arrests made the headlines—Mahmoud Khalil, a Palestinian organizer at Columbia University, and Rumeysa Ozturk, a Turkish doctoral student at Tufts University. Both were taken by immigration agents and sent to a detention center in Louisiana.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-265" id="fnref-265">265</a></sup></p>
<p>These attacks were politically motivated. Betar, a violent pro-Israel organization which was since sanctioned and shut down by New York,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-266" id="fnref-266">266</a></sup> has claimed that it had provided the Trump administration with thousands of names of people involved in Palestinian campus activism.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-267" id="fnref-267">267</a></sup></p>
<p>The ACLU and Amnesty International USA documented these forms of campus suppression in their September 2026 report, “Student Speech Under Attack: Suppression of Pro-Palestine Speech and Dissent on Campus”, which examined policies and practices at 80 public and private campuses across 28 states and the District of Columbia.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-268" id="fnref-268">268</a></sup></p>
<p>These attacks on faculty and students are not just an attack on expression of solidarity for Palestine, but also form an attack on scholarship about Palestine.</p>
<h3 id="s4-8-4">4.8.4 Reshaping U.S. Scholarship for Israel</h3>
<p>Multiple different types of suppression have reshaped U.S. universities: threats and cuts of federal funding by the government or intervention in academic hiring, cuts to programs, changes to university policies concerning antisemitism, and professional consequences for faculty who express solidarity with Palestine.</p>
<p>Academic institutions produce and disseminate research that is often cited by Wikipedia editors, and Wikipedia relies on published scholarship to support and verify its content. Suppression of scholarship on Palestine, including the closure or restriction of related courses and programs, could have an impact on the research produced about Palestine, and thus the sources available to Wikipedia editors, and ultimately what AI engines learn.</p>
