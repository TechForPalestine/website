<p>In order to understand why the ADL wants to control AI, and before closely examining the ADL’s own report in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6">Section 6</a>, it is necessary to first discuss the organization that produced it.</p>
<p>The Anti-Defamation League (ADL) claims it was founded in 1913 to protect the Jewish community from hate speech. However, it has long been regarded as a pro-Israel advocacy group and a member of the “Israel Lobby”. It is self-described by its CEO Jonathan Greenblatt as “a Zionist organization”, that is, an organization dedicated to supporting and advocating for Israel, and in particular Israel’s violent occupation of Palestine.</p>
<p>We show in this section that the ADL is not a civil rights organization, is widely regarded as racist,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-269" id="fnref-269">269</a></sup> and does not represent the rights of a marginalized community. It has “a long history of attacking Arab, Black, and queer people”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-270" id="fnref-270">270</a></sup> In fact, it even attacks other Jews, it feel they are not sufficiently pro-Israel, leading to the recent resignation of multiple ADL employees.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-271" id="fnref-271">271</a></sup></p>
<p>Rather, the ADL should be considered a pro-Israel propaganda organization, a core member of the Israel Lobby.</p>
<p>The ADL’s report should be understood through this lens: produced not for credible purposes that fit within their supposed mission of ending antisemitism, but rather as a propaganda piece that can be—and is being—weaponized to increase Israeli narratives and influence.</p>
<p>We support this assertion with evidence from both Jewish and non-Jewish civil society. We discuss how the ADL’s attempted redefinition of antisemitism has led to the false claims of a large increase in antisemitism in 2023, as researched by <em>Jewish Currents</em>.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-272" id="fnref-272">272</a></sup> We also discuss how Wikipedia banned the ADL due to being unreliable on the topic of Israel/Palestine, the very topic of this report.</p>
<p>We further discuss the ADL’s support of Elon Musk after his blatant antisemitism, as well as multiple resignations in protest of the ADL’s direction. We examine various critiques of the ADL, both from Jewish civil society and from other civil rights organizations, to show the extent of their pro-Israel propaganda.</p>
<p>Finally, we discuss the influence of CEO Jonathan Greenblatt’s leadership and his numerous problematic claims, including his own significant antisemitism against anti-Zionist Jews, and his connection and involvement with violent attacks against pro-Palestinian students at UCLA, where he was seen next to violent extremist groups.</p>
<h2 id="s5-1">5.1 The ADL and Zionism</h2>
<p>In 2008, the best-seller “the Israel Lobby” identified the ADL as one of the pillars of the Israel Lobby, alongside AIPAC and CAMERA.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-273" id="fnref-273">273</a></sup></p>
<p>The ADL was founded as a “Jewish defense organization”, and began to portray itself as a civil rights organiztion in the 1950s. Beginning in 1967, the ADL began to prioritize “advocacy on behalf of the State of Israel” and initiated an “education and action program to help mold public opinion” and to expose and counteract what they refer to as “Arab propaganda”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-274" id="fnref-274">274</a></sup></p>
<p>In the 1970s, the ADL attempted to define a “new antisemitism”, in which criticism of Israel would be recharacterized as antisemitic. They took the lead in “combating the Arab boycott of companies that do business with Israel”, which led to the passage of the 1977 and 1978 laws intended to “prohibit American companies from participating in the blacklist”, a key moment the ADL outlines in its own history.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-275" id="fnref-275">275</a></sup> From the late 1960s to the 1990s, the ADL ran an illegal surveillance network, in which it tracked critics of Israel including archbishop Desmond Tutu, alongside white supremacists.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-276" id="fnref-276">276</a></sup></p>
<p>Since the start of Jonathan Greenblatt’s leadership in 2015, the ADL has transformed into an organization dedicated to protecting Israel at all costs. As an ADL employee stated in 2024, “The ADL has a pro-Israel bias and an agenda to suppress pro-Palestinian activism”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-277" id="fnref-277">277</a></sup></p>
<p>In 2022, Greenblatt made a speech to ADL leadership in which he declared “anti-Zionism is antisemitism”,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-278" id="fnref-278">278</a></sup> and went further in 2025, publicly stating, “ADL is a Zionist organization, something for which not only will we never apologize for, we’ll never retreat”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-279" id="fnref-279">279</a></sup></p>
<p>At the ADL's 2022 annual leadership summit, Greenblatt focused his keynote address almost entirely on what the ADL calls “hostile anti-Zionist activist groups” such as Students for Justice in Palestine and Jewish Voice for Peace (groups protesting against Israel on American university campuses), and referred to them as “the photo inverse of the extreme right”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-280" id="fnref-280">280</a></sup></p>
<p>In fact, Greenblatt has shifted the ADL’s focus away from the far right. He said little about the rise of white Christian nationalism and its “replacement theory” that has inspired violence against Jews and others. He ignored antisemitic Republican campaign advertisements<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-281" id="fnref-281">281</a></sup> and Republican politicians appearing at neo-Nazi gatherings.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-282" id="fnref-282">282</a></sup> This is despite the fact that the ADL found that the anti-Zionist groups Greenblatt focused on were responsible for just 2 percent of antisemitic actions (of which 76% came from just one group).<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-283" id="fnref-283">283</a></sup></p>
<p>Not only that, but Greenblatt has in fact cosied up to the far right, platforming “far-right MAGA pastors and politicians, billionaire CEOs, and conservative journalists”. This mirrors the ADL of the 70s and 80s, opposing lessons on the KKK and affirmative action.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-284" id="fnref-284">284</a></sup></p>
<p>In January 2026, <em>The Forward</em> interviewed more than 20 people associated with the ADL, including Greenblatt and 13 other current and former employees, as well as public and private documents. In a resignation letter obtained by <em>The Forward</em>, former regional vice president Aaron Ahlquist argued that the ADL’s emphasis on defending Israel, alongside cuts to education protecting other minority groups, was marginalizing the organization. Former state and local policy manager Robert Sills said: “Core civil rights work is going away.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-285" id="fnref-285">285</a></sup></p>
<p>In a December 2022 commentary, Jewish Currents’ Noah Kulwin wrote:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“As a result of this contradictory mission, the ADL has permeated political discourse in the worst possible manner, using its moral authority to shield Israeli atrocities from criticism while offering the public misleading information about the nature and scale of contemporary Jew hatred. By conflating critique of Israel with antisemitism, the organization obscures the <em>actual</em> threat antisemitism poses as part of the vast constellation of bigotries channeled by the global right and abetted by institutional power.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-286" id="fnref-286">286</a></sup></p>
</blockquote>
<p>Kulwin went on to describe the “true personality” of the ADL as “a pro-Israel lobby with a civil rights hobby”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-287" id="fnref-287">287</a></sup></p>
<p>This prioritization of Israel advocacy over civil rights work has consequences. Rabbi Jill Jacobs, director of T’ruah, warned that the ADL’s retreat from work supporting other vulnerable groups and its antagonism toward former allies made it harder for Jewish organizations to persuade partners to take antisemitism seriously. Those allies, she said, “aren’t going to trust anything it says.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-288" id="fnref-288">288</a></sup></p>
<p>According to an ADL employee in 2024 who was interviewed by <em>The Guardian</em>, the organization’s conflation of antisemitism and anti-Zionism is damaging its efforts to counter hate; they say, “The ADL has a pro-Israel bias and an agenda to suppress pro-Palestinian activism”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-289" id="fnref-289">289</a></sup></p>
<p>The ADL's approach has become so one-sided that Wikipedia's community marked it as “unreliable”, prompting <em>The Forward</em> to call it a “wake-up call” for the organization.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-290" id="fnref-290">290</a></sup></p>
<h2 id="s5-2">5.2 Antisemitism Audit</h2>
<p>The ADL has consistently weaponized antisemitism in defense of Israel.</p>
<p>The ADL’s “Audit of Antisemitic Incidents 2023” revealed a 140% increase in antisemitism, a huge jump from 3,698 in 2022 to 8,873 in 2023.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-291" id="fnref-291">291</a></sup> The ADL used these numbers to aggressively push the narrative<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-292" id="fnref-292">292</a></sup> that antisemitism was on the rise, and used that narrative to demand that student protests of Israel’s genocide in Gaza be violently put down.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-293" id="fnref-293">293</a></sup></p>
<p>However, an analysis of these figures by <em>Jewish Currents</em> shows a dramatically different story.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-294" id="fnref-294">294</a></sup> As it turns out, in 2023 the ADL began labelling statements that criticize Israel’s occupation, apartheid, and genocide as antisemitic. Their 2023 audit was the first year they employed this updated definition of antisemitism, wherein they considered rallies that included “anti-Zionist chants and slogans” as antisemitic.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-295" id="fnref-295">295</a></sup></p>
<p><em>Jewish Currents</em> analyzed the report using the information provided by the ADL, and using the Jerusalem Declaration on Antisemitism,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-296" id="fnref-296">296</a></sup> which differentiates between the ADL’s definition of antisemitism and true antisemitism, which does not include criticism of Israel.</p>
<p>In the report, <em>Jewish Currents</em> found “more than a thousand items we believe were misclassified as antisemitic—all cases of speech critical of Israel or Zionism”. Not only that, but they “found that the data included misapplications of the organization’s own standards and often did not provide enough information for us to assess the group’s judgment”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-297" id="fnref-297">297</a></sup> which is an issue we’ll encounter in our own analysis of the ADL’s report on Wikipedia in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3">Section 6.3</a>.</p>
<figure class="report-figure"><img src="/images/wikipedia-report/08b8f0622c88.webp" alt="" width="799" height="499" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>The ADL’s report, according to <em>Jewish Currents</em>, recorded drawings of swastikas as antisemitic even when their connection to real antisemitism is questionable or non-existent, such as swastikas drawn as vandalism by teenagers, or swastikas drawn next to slurs targeting other minorities. One of the recorded cases specifically included a swastika drawn next to the phrase “F— Palestine”. They even include in their report “a swastika drawn in condensation on an empty restaurant table” and swastikas “scribbled alongside swear words or drawings of genitals”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-298" id="fnref-298">298</a></sup></p>
<p>The ADL also considered slogans of the Palestine movement as antisemitic. For example, more than 600 cases of the phrase ‘from the river to the sea’ were reported as antisemitic. This is supported by <em>The Forward's</em> note that out of the 3000 incidents of “antisemitism” the ADL claimed occurred since Israel’s invasion of Palestine, fully two-thirds were tied to Israel.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-299" id="fnref-299">299</a></sup> <em>Jewish Currents</em> concluded,</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“Our analysis clarifies what the ADL’s prominent report captures and excludes, and shows how the conflation of anti-Zionism and antisemitism skews the data—ultimately serving as a reminder of the need for serious statistical analysis done by an organization not beholden to Israel advocacy.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-300" id="fnref-300">300</a></sup></p>
</blockquote>
<p>This wasn’t the first time that the ADL used new methodologies to make their antisemitism numbers go up. They reported a massive 36% increase from 2021 to 2022, which <em>The New Republic</em> notes came not from a change of sentiment or actual increase in antisemitism, but, rather, from a change in methodology, explaining,</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“If you read the fine print in the footnotes of the organization’s press releases, though, you will learn that the ADL changed its counting method from previous years by partnering with a number of groups that provide it with more comprehensive data than it has had access to in the past.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-301" id="fnref-301">301</a></sup></p>
</blockquote>
<p>Even worse, the ADL’s report seems to have undercounted right-wing antisemitism. The ADL’s recent focus on Palestine protests comes at the expense of a focus on organizations who actually espouse hatred towards Jews. In the report, this appears to be a structural defect.</p>
<p>According to <em>Jewish Currents</em>, “White nationalist incidents that don’t explicitly name Jews are tracked as White Supremacist Propaganda or White Supremacist Events but not as Antisemitic Incidents, leaving them out of the audit”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-302" id="fnref-302">302</a></sup></p>
<p>Additionally, if white supremacist entries that were not originally included in the antisemitism audit were to be included, the total number of antisemitic incidents would rise to 15,564. According to <em>Jewish Currents</em>, “Since we found that most alleged antisemitic incidents in the Palestine solidarity movement lacked merit, the legitimately antisemitic Palestine-related incidents would appear as mere statistical noise when compared with the stunning growth of organized white nationalism”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-303" id="fnref-303">303</a></sup></p>
<p>This demonstrates not that antisemitism doesn’t exist, nor that it is not a problem, but rather that the ADL is weaponizing antisemitism—taking on absurd definitions where criticism of genocide is somehow antisemitic—to push back on valid criticism of Israel.</p>
<h2 id="s5-3">5.3 Civil Rights groups’ view of the ADL</h2>
<p>The ADL has received widespread condemnation for its policies and focus. Rather than being considered an ally by civil rights organizations throughout the U.S., and as we have shown in sections above, it is widely regarded as a single issue pro-Israel organization. Even worse, the ADL is regarded as having given up on any claimed legacy to civil rights or genuine work to combat antisemitism by the very groups which would be considered its allies otherwise.</p>
<h3 id="s5-3-1">5.3.1 #DropTheADL</h3>
<p>In August 2020, an open letter from various community and civil rights-focused organizations was circulated, addressing progressives across the U.S. to make clear that the ADL is not “an ally”, nor an organization that supported civil rights.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-304" id="fnref-304">304</a></sup> At the same time as the ADL had presented itself as “the premier civil rights group”, it was in fact spending its time “advancing pro-Israel US foreign policy and repressing the movement for Palestinian rights”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-305" id="fnref-305">305</a></sup></p>
<p>According to these groups, this represents part of a larger pattern; “Since the 1950s, the ADL has leveraged its strong media operation and its relationships with lawmakers both to quash Arab American community efforts to participate in US politics, and to defend Israel from widespread criticism of its system of segregation<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-306" id="fnref-306">306</a></sup> and apartheid.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-307" id="fnref-307">307</a></sup> The open letter also acknowledged how by protecting and defending Israel and its policies from criticism, the ADL “has supported and driven a neoconservative <a href="https://jewishvoiceforpeace.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/Network-Against-Islamophobia-FAQ.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">US-Israeli policy for colonization of the Middle East</a>.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-308" id="fnref-308">308</a></sup></p>
<p>The letter was signed by 287 civil rights groups, including advocacy groups for Black, Arab, LGBTQ, and Jewish rights. These include the Jewish groups IfNotNow, Jewish Voice for Peace, Jews Against Anti-Muslim Racism, Jews Against White Supremacy, Jews for Racial &amp; Economic Justice, The Jewish Vote, and Judaism On Our Own Terms. The letter was also signed by a range of other civil rights groups, including American Friends Service Committee, Center for Constitutional Rights, MediaJustice, National Lawyers Guild, and Showing Up for Racial Justice.</p>
<p>This large range of diverse groups claimed that the ADL portrays Israel and Zionism as part of “‘Jews’ civil rights struggle”, which then <strong>“</strong>makes Jewish rights seem tied to Israeli colonial conquest, and rationalizes Israel's anti-Palestinian, anti-Arab, and anti-Muslim racism as a 'civil rights' position.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-309" id="fnref-309">309</a></sup></p>
<p>These 287 groups have leveled serious and well-documented charges against the ADL, which is explained in depth within the #DropTheADL primer, “The ADL is not an ally”. With the intent of exposing the ADL’s lesser-known, and ongoing, legacy of “supporting racist policing, surveillance, colonialism, and the silencing of social justice activism”, the signees detail the ADL’s history of targeting and surveilling progressive movements, repressing Palestinian rights, labeling criticism as antisemitic, employing Islamophobic/anti-Muslim racism, supporting right-wing antisemitic and racist influencers, engaging in university campus repression, and attempting to attack other social justice-oriented movements.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-310" id="fnref-310">310</a></sup></p>
<h3 id="s5-3-2">5.3.2 Wikipedia labels the ADL as “generally unreliable”</h3>
<p>In April 2024, dozens of Wikipedia editors began discussing whether the ADL could be considered a reliable source to be included in Wikipedia.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-311" id="fnref-311">311</a></sup> Focussing on the ADL’s conduct since October 7th (although the history of the ADL, as shown, is just as revealing), the group concluded that the ADL acted primarily as a pro-Israel advocacy group, and conflated legitimate criticism of Israel with antisemitism.</p>
<p>They specifically referred to the ADL’s changes in methodology to their Antisemitism Audit, and its dual role as both an advocacy and research organization. In a June 2024 vote, Wikipedians declared that the ADL was a “generally unreliable” source on issues of antisemitism or Israel’s war on Gaza.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-312" id="fnref-312">312</a></sup></p>
<p>In a quote to the <em>Jewish Telegraphic Agency</em>, Mira Sucharov, a professor of political science at Carleton University, said “This is a sign that the Jewish community needs better institutions”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-313" id="fnref-313">313</a></sup> The Forward called it a “wake-up call”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-314" id="fnref-314">314</a></sup></p>
<h3 id="s5-3-3">5.3.3 The “Glossary of Extremism”</h3>
<p>After the killing of Charlie Kirk, leader of Turning Point USA, in September 2025, FBI Director Kash Patel announced that the FBI was severing all ties with the ADL, due to its inclusion of Turning Point USA within the ADL’s “Glossary of Extremism”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-315" id="fnref-315">315</a></sup></p>
<p>Given that the ADL considered this group worthy of inclusion in their “Glossary of Extremism”, alongside many other right-wing groups—many of whom are genuinely antisemitic—we would expect the ADL to take a principled approach against this response.</p>
<p>Of course not. Instead, the ADL deleted its entire “Glossary of Extremism”, which contained over 1,000 entries of organizations, individuals, and phrases covering decades of research into hatred across the U.S., claiming that it was “outdated”. The ADL had first debuted this glossary as recently as 2022, positioning it as a “first-of-its-kind” database to understand extremism.</p>
<h3 id="s5-3-4">5.3.4 Reinstating Apartheid Airbnb Rentals</h3>
<p>In 2018, after a <em>Human Rights Watch</em> report called on Airbnb to end rentals on illegally-settled land in the West Bank, Airbnb made the decision to actually end those rentals.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-316" id="fnref-316">316</a></sup></p>
<p>The ADL issued a response in which they suggested that Airbnb was complicit in antisemitism, and began a (successful) campaign<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-317" id="fnref-317">317</a></sup> to reinstate the stolen land rentals.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-318" id="fnref-318">318</a></sup></p>
<p>Writing in Jewish Currents, Joshua Leifer identified the ADL’s effort to make Airbnb reverse its decision on West Bank settlement listings as part of their broader defense of Israeli settlement policy.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-319" id="fnref-319">319</a></sup> He criticized the ADL’s positions as “shielding Israel from criticism or consequences for violating international law.”</p>
<p>This demonstrates, once again, that the ADL puts its advocacy for Israel above civil rights, and weaponizes antisemitism to achieve its goals.</p>
<h3 id="s5-3-5">5.3.5 The National Education Association</h3>
<p>On July 6, 2025, the National Education Association, the largest teachers’ association in the U.S., representing approximately three million educators, voted to sanction the ADL and to refuse to use any materials from the ADL.</p>
<p>The National Education Association stated, “despite its reputation as a civil rights organization, the ADL is not the social justice educational partner it claims to be”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-320" id="fnref-320">320</a></sup> Speakers rallied against the ADL’s weaponization of the term “antisemitism”, the false statistics on hate crimes, and the characterization of pro-Palestinian liberation speech as “hate speech”. According to one delegate, “Allowing the ADL to determine what constitutes antisemitism would be like allowing the fossil fuel industry to determine what constitutes climate change”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-321" id="fnref-321">321</a></sup></p>
<p>This came partially in response to the ADL attacking NEA constituents who had developed resources for educators themselves to learn about the history of Palestine. Labor Notes reported that the ADL “cherry-picked elements to claim that presenting Palestinian perspectives amounted to ‘glorifying terrorists’”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-322" id="fnref-322">322</a></sup></p>
<p>In the end, over 100 civil rights organizations<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-323" id="fnref-323">323</a></sup> endorsed the NEA’s #DroptheADLFromSchools resources for educators.</p>
<h2 id="s5-4">5.4 Abandoning Anti-Hate Work</h2>
<p>In March 2025, Danielle Bryant, former Education director for the ADL's Austin, Texas chapter, published an Op Ed in the <em>Daily News</em> entitled “The ADL goes quiet on some hatreds”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-324" id="fnref-324">324</a></sup> In it, she recounted myriad changes to the ADL’s education programs, dropping anti-racism and anti-hatred work, commenting on her recognition of how the ADL “weaponized its influence and quietly stepped away from commitments to justice”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-325" id="fnref-325">325</a></sup> She went on to explain,</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“I watched from the inside as the ADL erased racial justice from its civil rights priorities, caved to pressure from conservative media for being ‘too woke,’<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-326" id="fnref-326">326</a></sup> and quietly abandoned core education programs. The shift began with an internal pause on its use of the word ‘racism’<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-327" id="fnref-327">327</a></sup> while it sought a “new” definition (spoiler: they still don’t have one).<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-328" id="fnref-328">328</a></sup> Then, it removed ‘racial justice’ from its portfolio altogether.</p>
<p>“Rather than expand anti-bias education, it shut down a decades-old initiative that helped students challenge discrimination. Then, it removed more than 100 lesson plans that helped educators address bias against LGBTQ, Muslim, and immigrant students.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-329" id="fnref-329">329</a></sup></p>
</blockquote>
<p>Indeed, in September 2025, the ADL even took offline their Glossary of Extremism, a glossary of over 1000 terms documenting extremism which they had launched in 2022. This was in response to attacks from right-wing figures such as Elon Musk for listing Christian extremist groups in the Glossary.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-330" id="fnref-330">330</a></sup></p>
<p>The ADL was later found to have removed “Protect Civil Rights” from its mission.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-331" id="fnref-331">331</a></sup></p>
<p>It is clear that the ADL has had a significant shift away from anti-discrimination work, instead focusing on attacking the Palestine movement in order to support Israel. Bryant also argues that, by shielding Israel from scrutiny while giving cover to far-right extremists, the ADL “undermines the fight against antisemitism.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-332" id="fnref-332">332</a></sup></p>
<h2 id="s5-5">5.5 High-Profile ADL Resignations</h2>
<p>Over the last few years, due to the ADL’s increasing embrace of right-wing extremism and attacks on defenders of Palestinian rights, a number of staff have publicly resigned.</p>
<p>In October 2023, Stephen C. Rea, a senior researcher at the ADL’s Center for Technology &amp; Society, resigned. He stated that he could not square his “morals and politics” with the direction the organization was going, specifically objecting to Greenblatt's condemnation of American Jews protesting Israel’s war in Gaza.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-333" id="fnref-333">333</a></sup></p>
<p>In March 2025, Joe Berman, a former chair of the ADL’s National Legal Affairs Committee, resigned from its national and regional leadership bodies, citing the ADL’s endorsement of Mahmoud Khalil’s detention as his breaking point. His resignation letter accused the ADL of providing political cover for the Trump administration, warning that concern about antisemitism was being exploited to advance “an authoritarian, anti-democratic agenda”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-334" id="fnref-334">334</a></sup></p>
<p>As discussed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-5#s5-4">Section 5.4</a> above, in March 2025, Danielle Bryant, former Education director for the ADL's Austin, Texas chapter, published an Op Ed entitled “The ADL goes quiet on some hatreds”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-335" id="fnref-335">335</a></sup> In it, she recounted a myriad of changes from the ADL embracing the right-wing, and how the ADL “erased racial justice from its civil rights priorities”. Bryant eventually resigned in protest after the ADL presented Jared Kushner an award for his work on the Abraham Accords at the 2024 conference.</p>
<p>In May 2025, Steven Ludwig, who had been a board member of the ADL’s Philadelphia chapter for over 30 years, resigned in protest of the ADL’s recent actions.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-336" id="fnref-336">336</a></sup> In a scathing seven-page letter, Ludwig criticized the ADL’s turn away from civil rights and its support for the Trump administration’s attacks on free speech and civil society, saying “the ADL has failed to stand up against the spread of hatred, the erosion of the rule of law, and the threat of authoritarianism”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-337" id="fnref-337">337</a></sup></p>
<h2 id="s5-6">5.6 ADL Support for Elon Musk</h2>
<p>The ADL has recently been a staunch supporter of Elon Musk, despite his propensity towards antisemitism. In fact, the ADL is often the one defending Musk’s antisemitism.</p>
<p>In November 2022, after Elon Musk took over Twitter (now X), the ADL launched an advertising boycott of X, at the same time as there was an alleged 60% increase in hate speech on the platform.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-338" id="fnref-338">338</a></sup> They paused this boycott after a few months, and continued to advertise on X themselves.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-339" id="fnref-339">339</a></sup></p>
<p>In September 2023, Musk threatened to sue the ADL, taking part in the #BanTheADL campaign.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-340" id="fnref-340">340</a></sup> And in November 2023, Musk replied, “You have said the actual truth” in response to a post claiming Jewish people “have been pushing the exact kind of dialectical hatred against whites that they claim to want people to stop using against them”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-341" id="fnref-341">341</a></sup> He further accused ‘Jewish communities’, the Anti-Defamation League, and minorities of ‘anti-white’ messaging, as well as agreeing with “antisemitic conspiracy theory”. Greenblatt called Musk’s comment “indisputably dangerous”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-342" id="fnref-342">342</a></sup></p>
<p>However, the ADL soon softened significantly on Musk, despite Musk continuing to promote antisemitism. In November 2023, Musk visited Israel, where according to reports, he was fed the Israeli perspective through meeting with government officials, an Israeli military spokesperson, and being shown an Israeli montage video of October 7th.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-343" id="fnref-343">343</a></sup> Musk also met with Netanyahu. In January 2024, Musk’s antisemitism apology tour continued with a visit to Auschwitz, alongside pro-Israel commentator Ben Shapiro.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-344" id="fnref-344">344</a></sup></p>
<p>At the same time, Musk created a number of policies that censored speech pertaining to Palestinian liberation.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-345" id="fnref-345">345</a></sup> He announced that the phrase “from the river to the sea” and the word “decolonization” could lead to suspension from X. This led to significant support from Greenblatt, who said “I appreciate this leadership in fighting hate”.</p>
<p>Commenting on X, <em>New Yorker</em> writer Isaac Chotiner wrote, “The most prominent organization fighting anti-Semitism in America will commend your ‘leadership in fighting hate’ 24 hours after you endorse vile neo-Nazi anti-Semitism…if you take a strong stand against critics of Israel”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-346" id="fnref-346">346</a></sup></p>

<p>The ADL’s embrace of Musk further demonstrates that the focus of Greenblatt, and the ADL at large, is <em>not</em> on battling antisemitism, but rather on shielding Israel from any form of criticism.</p>

<p>The Sisters of St. Francis of Philadelphia, a group of nuns in “service to the needs of others, especially the economically poor, the marginal, and the oppressed”,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-360" id="fnref-360">360</a></sup> called out Lockheed Martin and their $3 billion deal to sell 25 F-35s to Israel, saying “F-35s have been used repeatedly by Israeli forces to target Palestinian civilians in Gaza and are connected to apparent war crimes”, and that this violated Lockheed’s “Human Rights Policy, commits to protecting and advancing human rights and minimizing the negative consequences of its business activities”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-361" id="fnref-361">361</a></sup></p>
