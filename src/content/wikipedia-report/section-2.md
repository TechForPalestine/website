<p>These LLMs are already biased against the human rights of the Palestinian people. A study by Google Deepmind, “Do LLMs exhibit demographic parity in responses to queries about Human Rights?”, reveals anti-Palestinian racism has already infected Large Language Models.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-30" id="fnref-30">30</a></sup></p>

<h3 id="s2-3-2">2.3.2 IHRA Definition</h3>
<p>The International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance’s (IHRA) “Working Definition of Antisemitism” is one of the primary tools most leveraged to conflate criticism of Israel with antisemitism, providing a framework used to censor and criminalize Palestinian speech and content.</p>

European Monitoring Centre on Racism and Xenophobia (EUMC), which published a draft of the definition on its website in 2005<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-46" id="fnref-46">46</a></sup>. Significant problems were raised with the document, and in 2013, the EU Agency for Fundamental Rights—successor of the EUMC—clarified that the draft was never adopted, and removed it from its website.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-47" id="fnref-47">47</a></sup></p>
<p>In 2014, the U.S.-based Simon Wiesenthal Center, an organization with the goal of defending Zionism,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-48" id="fnref-48">48</a></sup> proposed that the IHRA, an international task force on Holocaust Remembrance founded in 1998 by the U.S., U.K., and Sweden<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-49" id="fnref-49">49</a></sup>, adopt the definition under its own brand<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-50" id="fnref-50">50</a></sup>. <p>Tech for Palestine’s Media Bias Meter project interrogated media bias, discussing this widespread bias in mainstream media.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-62" id="fnref-62">62</a></sup> In a recent report, Media Bias Meter engaged in a comparative analysis of media bias in eight major Western media outlets, finding that, overall and overwhelmingly, headline bias, omission bias, and stereotyping and loaded language prioritizing pro-Israel positions, and Israeli-centric framings of events, are dominant.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-63" id="fnref-63">63</a></sup></p>
<p>They found that the New York Times mentioned Israel 186 times more frequently than Palestine in headlines. They found that 80 of the BBC’s 91 mentions of Palestine were in reference to Palestine Action, pro-Palestine demonstrations, or graffiti, rather than Palestine itself.</p>
<p>A similar report from Tech for Palestine affiliate Newscord found that the BBC used passive voice to nearly 80% of sentences describing Palestinian casualties, omitting Israel’s involvement.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-64" id="fnref-64">64</a></sup> The BBC, Sky News, and The Guardian attributed attacks to Israel in only 50%, 54%, and 54% of relevant cases, respectively, while Al Jazeera did so in 89% of cases.</p>
<p>A report from the Centre for Media Monitoring<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-65" id="fnref-65">65</a></sup> have similar findings. When faked atrocity porn related to “beheaded babies” was spreading across media, only 52 of 361 television clips questioned, challenged, or rebutted the claims. These are claims which we know now never had any evidence behind them, but were accepted at face value across the media landscape.</p>
<p>Another report from the Centre for Media Monitoring<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-66" id="fnref-66">66</a></sup> also found that the BBC devoted 34 times as much coverage per fatality to Israel deaths than Palestinian deaths. Israeli deaths were also far frequently humanized, sharing their names, photographs, and relationships. In fact, the BBC also used the word “murder” 220 times for Israeli victims, but only once for Palestinian victims.</p>
<p>A new book “Don't Say Palestine: How the Media Manufactured Consent for Genocide”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-67" id="fnref-67">67</a></sup> demonstrates how the media tweaks headlines and selects articles that are favorable to Israel, and that dehumanize, or are biased against Palestinians. Author Assal Rad gives examples of hospitals being “squeezed” rather than being systematically destroyed by Israeli bombardment; of Palestinians in East Jerusalem being “evicted”—using the language of ordinary landlord/tenant disputes, rather than being forcefully displaced from their homes.</p>
<p>In particular, Rad reported how CNN, the BBC, and the New York Times had all reported that Hind Rajab, the 5-year old Palestinian girl who was trapped in a car after her family were killed by Israeli soldiers, was “found dead” after she too was killed by the Israeli army. The car carrying Rajab and her family had been shot by hundreds of bullets, and the rescue workers sent to save her were also killed by the Israeli military.</p>
<p>In a demonstration of how this sort of bias occurs, Drop Site News interviewed 13 BBC journalists and other staffers,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-68" id="fnref-68">68</a></sup> revealing how the BBC systematically “skewed stories in favor of Israel’s narratives and repeatedly dismissed objections registered by scores of staffers who, throughout the past 14 months, demanded that the network uphold its commitment to impartiality and fairness”.</p>
<p>Their reporting revealed that BBC’s Middle East online editor Raffi Berg watered down reporting critical of Israel. Berg had previously written multiple articles praising the Israeli military, as well as a book in which he described the Mossad “in glowing terms”.</p>
