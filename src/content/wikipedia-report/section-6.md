<p>Many of the influence attacks on Wikipedia rely on and reference the ADL report entitled “Editing for Hate: How Anti-Israel and Anti-Jewish Bias Undermines Wikipedia’s Neutrality” (March 2025). This is the central document used in the influence attacks on Wikipedia, which we discussed in Sections <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-1">4.1</a>, <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-2">4.2</a>, <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-3">4.3</a>, and <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-4">4.4</a>.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-1">Section 6.1</a>, we discuss the core issues of the report, namely that the evidence it presents is poor, and that it fails to prove any of their claims. These flaws, combined with a lack of engagement with Wikipedia on these issues before the report’s publication, demonstrates that the ADL is not making a good-faith attempt, as they claim, to reduce hate. Rather, it makes clear that their goal is to weaponize the report to gain influence over Wikipedia.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-2">Section 6.2</a> we discuss an article published in <em>The Forward</em> by Dr. Shira Klein, an expert on antisemitism and Wikipedia. Dr. Klein’s research was referenced in the ADL’s report, and her article publicly refutes the ADL’s claims.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-389" id="fnref-389">389</a></sup> In the article, she states that Wikipedia is extremely reliable on the topic of Israel and Palestine, contradicting the ADL’s assertions.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3">Section 6.3</a>, we systematically go through the ADL report’s claims of collusion in Case Study 1, where we show the report is filled with fallacies, contradictory evidence, and unverifiable accusations that rely on biased definitions and fake-scientific terminology. We demonstrate that the report is not the scholarly work it claims to be, but is a deeply flawed and embarrassing piece of propaganda, which doesn’t merit U.S. government attention or to be addressed by Wikipedia.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-4">Section 6.4</a>, we apply the same process to Case Study 2, the ADL’s claims about antisemitism in the Arabic Language section of Wikipedia. The ADL appears to have made the severe error of relying on English-language translations, which add incorrect nuance which isn’t there in the original. They claim that neutral passages imply praise for Hamas, without explanation. They also claim bias and antisemitism in simple and factual descriptions of Palestinian history which does not suit their narrative.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-5">Section 6.5</a>, we continue this systematic analysis of a follow-up report by the ADL, called “Damage Done: Wikipedia Leaves Biased Edits Unchecked Despite Editor Bans”. In it, the ADL celebrates its success from manipulating Wikipedia along with their allies. They make further false claims which identify a supposed new cohort of antisemitic editing, and restate old recommendations and make new ones, in order to gain more influence over the platform. We dissect this report, methodically examining its spin and narrative claims that are once again unsupported by evidence, as well as its goals and propaganda purpose.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-6">Section 6.6</a>, we address the “systematic edits” claims made by the ADL, which were repeated in the attacks from numerous politicians. The ADL claims that Tech for Palestine (authors of this report), created a mass-editing campaign. We demonstrate how the ADL spun this false narrative, citing data which itself demonstrates that no such mass-editing campaign exists. This shows how the ADL spins false stories into being, in order to tell a story that can be better weaponized by allies in Congress.</p>
<p>Finally, in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-7">Section 6.7</a>, we go through the “Recommendations” made by the ADL to the US government, to Wikipedia, and to AI Labs creating LLMs. We go through each recommendation, explaining how the specific recommendations have not been backed up by the ADL’s fabricated findings.</p>
<h2 id="s6-1">6.1 The Reports’ Core Flaws</h2>
<p>In this section we present the core flaws with the ADL’s report.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-390" id="fnref-390">390</a></sup> These are based off of our detailed analysis of the ADL’s reports, which we present in Sections <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3">6.3</a>, <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-4">6.4</a>, and <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-5">6.5</a>.</p>
<h3 id="s6-1-1">6.1.1 The invented group</h3>
<p>The core of the ADL’s argument is that it found 30 Wikipedia users who are conspiring to create anti-Israel and antisemitic bias.</p>
<p>The major problem with the ADL’s narrative is that it is just a story. They are telling a story of a group of 30 editors who they claim are colluding. But these 30 people are not people the ADL, or anybody else, have seen colluding. They have not seen them in a room together (nor even a Discord channel). The grouping is simply one that the ADL has invented.</p>
<p>The alleged members of this conspiracy almost certainly do not actually know each other, are not friends, and have never spoken in real-life. The only record of communication, or even the existence, of these people, is via public Wikipedia pages.</p>
<p>The ADL wants you to think it found 30 antisemites in a room, conspiring to stick it to the Jews. But the ADL have not found anything to connect these people apart from edits on Wikipedia. They don’t have the basics of a conspiracy. Who is the organizer? Where are their instructions? When or where do they meet? The ADL does not have any of this information, almost certainly because it does not exist.</p>
<p>(We note that the ADL and several pro-Israel organizations have attempted to say that these editors colluded on a specific Discord channel. This is provably false due to the dates and quantity of edits involved, as we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-6">Section 6.6</a>.)</p>
<p>This group of 30 editors is an invention of the ADL. They took Wikipedia data—usernames and edits—and they decided to connect these 30 people in order to be able to tell a story about them. But the story is just that—a story. It is not rooted in the real world.</p>
<p>In each part of <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6">Section 6</a>, we show how flimsy the evidence is which the ADL presents. But it’s important to remember that the entire narrative is simply a mirage which the ADL created, based on (bad) statistical inferences of data they found in Wikipedia datasets.</p>
<p>The simple fact is the ADL knows nothing about these people, or their relationship, in the real world. There is no evidence that they know each other, or that they have ever spoken, and that is because they almost certainly have not. These are simply 30 separate Wikipedia editors who have one shared topic of interest, and that is all the ADL actually knows.</p>
<p>We contrast this to the seventeen actually demonstrated conspiracies to add pro-Israel slants to Wikipedia, which we outlined in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-3">Section 3</a>.</p>
<h3 id="s6-1-2">6.1.2 Unfalsifiable evidence</h3>
<p>A key problem with the ADL’s story is that it is not “falsifiable”. That is, it cannot be demonstrated to be incorrect.</p>
<p>When the editors work on Palestine-Israel articles, the ADL says that they’re conspiring to add antisemitism to Wikipedia. But when they don’t work on Palestine-Israel Articles, the ADL says they’re covering their tracks. When they communicate on Wikipedia, the ADL says they’re colluding. When they don’t communicate on Wikipedia, the ADL says they’re colluding off-platform.</p>
<p>The ADL’s goal was to produce a narrative, and every fact, no matter what it actually says, is woven into the conspiracy.</p>
<p>In fact, the ADL had none of the basic elements of a properly-conducted data analysis, such as stating their hypotheses, defining the terms they use, presenting confidence intervals alongside the data, controlling for metrics such as account age or sample size. In particular, they made no effort to provide reproducibility (in fact, frequently hiding sources during their most egregious manipulation of the results).</p>
<h3 id="s6-1-3">6.1.3 Unsupported accusations of antisemitism</h3>
<p>The ADL repeatedly invoked anti-Jewish bias and antisemitism, but did not even attempt to demonstrate it. In fact, nearly every claim about content referred specifically to content about Israel and Palestine.</p>
<p>The ADL’s main claim is of antisemitism, but despite that claim, they presented no evidence that their editors had any bias against Jews, or had inserted any anti-Jewish information on Wikipedia. Instead, the ADL attempted to demonstrate anti-Israel bias, and then made the leap to antisemitism without any additional evidence.</p>
<p>Despite spending a significant amount of time claiming that editors were antisemitic, including claiming “deceptive, manipulative practices” “targeted at entries related to Jews and Israel”, bias in ‘pages on “Israel, Jews, and the Palestinians”’, editors who are “anti-Jewish and anti-Israel”, and alleging “Wikipedia needs to do far more to address anti-Jewish and anti-Israel bias and coordination”, the ADL’s supposed evidence barely even looked at Judaism, Jewish communities (that is, not including Israel), or Jewish people.</p>
<p>In fact, the ADL didn’t actually make a lot of accusations about antisemitism at all. One accusation they made, discussed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-5-5">Section 6.3.4.5.5</a> was about removing a passage about a single Palestinian flying a swastika kite which was removed due to “Undue weight”.</p>
<p>The other accusations, which we discuss in Sections <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-5-6">6.3.4.5.6</a> and <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-5-7">6.3.4.5.7</a>, were facts that were incredibly well supported by academic research, the bedrock of Wikipedia content. In one of the cases, the ADL actually distorted the quote by removing key content.</p>
<h3 id="s6-1-4">6.1.4 No false anti-Israel information was shown to be added</h3>
<p>It is certainly true that Wikipedia contains a lot of information about Israel’s long history of crimes against humanity, but the ADL did not even attempt to show that any of it was false. In fact, they only at one point claimed that information on Wikipedia was factually incorrect, where the ADL disagreed with Wikipedia’s definition of Zionism. The claim with which they disagreed was supported by 17 references to scholarly works, many by Israeli Jewish historians.</p>
<p>Indeed, the ADL does a poor job of demonstrating that supposed anti-Israel facts are false. Wikipedia has a very high standard of evidence, relying principally on peer-reviewed scholarship. Facts unsupported by evidence rarely stay on the site, especially on controversial pages.</p>
<p>The ADL does attempt to claim biased edits, but it does not claim that those edits are incorrect. We address each of these supposedly biased edits in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-5">Section 6.3.4.5</a>, in which we demonstrated serious flaws with each of the ADL claims, none of which we believe are substantiated. The ADL’s “Manipulation and Disinformation”, which we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4">Section 6.3.4</a>, also does not allege falsehoods.</p>
<p>This is a key flaw with the ADL’s case: they attempted to show simply that facts critical of Israel were added. At no point did they show these editors manipulated Wikipedia to add false information.</p>
<h3 id="s6-1-5">6.1.5 No divergence from scholarly opinion</h3>
<p>To show actual anti-Israel bias, the ADL would need to demonstrate that information was introduced which contradicts peer-reviewed literature—the gold standard on Wikipedia.</p>
<p>As Dr. Shira Klein says:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“But where is the evidence of editors repeatedly misrepresenting or contradicting peer-reviewed literature? There is none. The report simply wants us to take the ADL’s word for it”.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>“The ADL’s main claim is that a group of 30 “bad faith editors” are working in tandem to “minimize Palestinian violence and terrorism while delegitimizing Israel”. But to a scholar’s eye, the evidence the group offers to support this claim is lacking. To establish foul play, the ADL would need to demonstrate that Wikipedia content about Israel and Zionism regularly expresses as fact ideas that diverge from broadly held scholarly opinions on the matters in question. (When edited well, Wikipedia summarizes scholarly consensus, substantiating every claim with peer-reviewed literature.)”</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="s6-1-6">6.1.6 Severe methodological flaws</h3>
<h4 id="s6-1-6-1">6.1.6.1 Working backwards from a predetermined conclusion</h4>
<p>Their method in Case Study 1, which we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-1">Section 6.3.1</a>, was to work backwards from a pre-determined conclusion: they chose the editors they didn’t like, then attempted to find evidence to implicate them. This Stalinesque approach—“Show me the man and I’ll show you the crime”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-391" id="fnref-391">391</a></sup>—involved cherry-picking a tiny amount of evidence from the over 1,000,000 edits made by the editors, which they attempted to spin to implicate the editors. As shown in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3">Section 6.3</a>, the evidence they used for this was also incredibly flawed and failed to demonstrate even the spin they sought.</p>
<p>Frequently, the ADL’s evidence is circular. They will define a group of editors based on some criteria, and then use either that criteria, or a derivative of it, as proof of malicious editing.</p>
<p>As discussed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-2-2">Section 6.3.2.2</a>, the ADL’s chosen “bad-faith” editors, who had been selected for being frequent editors of key pages, were claimed to be colluding for participation in discussions in those pages.</p>
<p>Similarly, as discussed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-2-3">Section 6.3.2.3</a>, the ADL claimed that editors were suspicious for being frequent editors of some articles, when in fact the ADL had chosen them because they were frequent editors of those articles.</p>
<p>As we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-2-4">Section 6.3.2.4</a>, the ADL selected the editors for being frequent editors. They attempted to use “time between edits” as proof of bad-faith, when clearly more frequent editors will have a lower average time between edits, as frequency and time-between-edits are mathematical inverses.</p>
<h4 id="s6-1-6-2">6.1.6.2 Manufacturing the appearance of collusion</h4>
<p>The ADL makes consistent claims of collusion, but the evidence to support it does not exist. As we showed in 6.1.6.1, most of the attributes they claim are actually just reflections of the criteria they used to group the editors.</p>
<p>Beyond that, as we show in detail in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-2">Section 6.3.2</a>, they produce a number of attributes: “editing frequency”, “group communication”, “tandem editing”, “time between edits”. However, they do not actually establish why we should consider these traits to demonstrate collusion. They do not have evidence that colluding editors have these traits, and present no reason to think that they would.</p>
<h4 id="s6-1-6-3">6.1.6.3 Framing ordinary Wikipedia activity as sinister</h4>
<p>As we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4">Section 6.3.4</a>, the ADL frames ordinary editing behaviour as being sinister, claiming that editing outside of the Palestine-Israel articles is "camouflage" and that making minor edits is “gaming the system”. These activities are normal activities that every Wikipedia editor does, and indeed is encouraged to do. They claim that a cumulative million edits between 30 editors is a lot, despite the fact that 20 Wikipedia editors are known to have a million edits each.</p>
<p>They even suggest editors are paid to work on it, using data which decidedly does not show anyone working steadily 9-5.</p>
<h4 id="s6-1-6-4">6.1.6.4 Cherry-picked evidence</h4>
<p>The ADL’s evidence was cherry-picked to attempt to show things which supported the ADL’s narrative. The ADL’s targeted editors had contributed a million edits to Wikipedia, and from that massive corpus they selected a number of articles, stripped them of context, thus constructing the evidence to support that narrative.</p>
<p>Given a million context-stripped edits from any group of people, we have no doubt the ADL could make them say anything. However, the removed context in each case demonstrated that the ADL was lying about what these cherry-picked articles say.</p>
<p>The fact that none of the context-stripped edits held up makes clear that the ADL’s case is built on nothing at all. We discuss most of these edits in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4">Section 6.3.4</a>.</p>
<h4 id="s6-1-6-5">6.1.6.5 Lack of Wikimedia consultation</h4>
<p>It also appears they did not take their evidence to Wikimedia before publication, who could have helped correct the issues.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-392" id="fnref-392">392</a></sup> This shows the report was not a genuine attempt to help guide Wikipedia, but rather a propaganda tool for the influence operation discussed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4">Section 4</a>.</p>
<p>Wikimedia made a small statement in response to a query from CNN, saying “It is unfortunate that we were not asked by the report’s authors to provide information and context that might have helped allay some of the concerns raised”.</p>
<p>That is to say, the ADL made serious and significant allegations about Wikipedia without once contacting the organization behind it. Given that the ADL makes a significant set of recommendations to Wikimedia about changes to make, it is quite shocking that it did not engage them for a simple comment before publishing, especially since engaging with organizations is a common activity in which the ADL engages.</p>
<h3 id="s6-1-7">6.1.7 Manipulation and misrepresentation of evidence</h3>
<p>Repeatedly throughout their reports, the ADL manipulated and misrepresented data, through misleading descriptions, selective quotation, hiding context, and withholding the links which would demonstrate otherwise.</p>
<h4 id="s6-1-7-1">6.1.7.1 Distorting evidence through misleading descriptions and selective quotation</h4>
<p>The ADL committed several gross violations of integrity in their report, when they deliberately distorted evidence. The most egregious, which we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-5-7">Section 6.3.4.5.7</a>, is when they distorted a quote by removing the second half, which contradicted what the ADL attempted to make it show.</p>
<p>Similarly, it intentionally misconstrued the contents of the Palestine Remembered website, discussed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-6">Section 6.3.6</a>, by claiming that a Frequently Asked Question—which they subsequently answered in contradiction to the ADL’s claim—was in fact a statement made by the site owners.</p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-5-1">Section 6.3.4.5.1</a>, we also discuss how the ADL claimed evidence was removed, when in fact it had simply been moved and then actually expanded.</p>
<h4 id="s6-1-7-2">6.1.7.2 Ignoring or hiding edit descriptions which undermined their narrative</h4>
<p>Every change on Wikipedia is supported by an edit description, in which the editor provides context for the change. Frequently, the ADL claimed a biased reason for something which was simply and adequately explained by the edit. The ADL should have, but never did, explain why their reasoning was stronger than the stated reason for the edit, and only in a handful of cases did the ADL even acknowledge the editor’s stated reason for doing an edit.</p>
<p>Instead, the ADL simply claimed that edits were for the purpose of “anti-Israel bias”, and concocted fabricated stories that were frequently directly contradicted by the context the ADL deliberately omitted.</p>
<h4 id="s6-1-7-3">6.1.7.3 Withholding the links needed to scrutinize its claims</h4>
<p>Wikipedia provides “permalinks” to each edit made by an editor on the platform. Each of the ADL’s claims should have been backed by a link to the exact change, which could be used to validate the claim. Instead, they were omitted in nearly every single case. See Sections <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-5">6.3.4.5</a> and <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-6">6.3.4.6</a> for major examples and discussion.</p>
<p>We believe the ADL deliberately omitted this valuable context. When we retrieved the approximately 100 actual source edits which the ADL hid, they almost always undermined the ADL’s point and demonstrated a different situation from the ADL’s narrative. We have included those change links in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3">Section 6.3</a> as we expose the significant flaws in their work.</p>
<h3 id="s6-1-8">6.1.8 Claims contradicted by the ADL’s own evidence</h3>

<h4 id="s6-1-8-1">6.1.8.1 Supposedly suspicious editing patterns also appear among other editors</h4>

<h4 id="s6-1-8-2">6.1.8.2 The ADL’s accusations are undermined by source data</h4>

<h2 id="s6-3">6.3 Detailed Critique of Case Study 1</h2>
<p>The bulk of the ADL’s report and evidence are in Case Study 1, in which they claim there is a group of editors who are making “coordinated anti-Jewish, anti-Israel bias and manipulation”, specifically that there is “bad-faith coordination [...] to amplify criticism of Israel and minimize evidence of violence and antisemitism by Palestinian terrorist groups”.</p>
<p>They make three claims:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>These editors are “bad-faith” or “suspicious”</p></li>
<li><p>These editors are “coordinating” their edits</p></li>
<li><p>These editors are putting misinformation on Wikipedia</p></li>
</ul>
<p>In fact, they showed no such thing. Their criteria of “bad-faith” editors was simply editors who contributed facts and made arguments that the ADL did not like. They then tried to identify some attributes about their new grouping of editors, and claimed that some of these attributes—such as making a lot of edits, or working on similar pages—were evidence of coordination.</p>
<p>To reiterate: the ADL’s investigation starts by selecting their bad-faith editors, then attempts to find reasons to support the grouping they have already picked. They decided who the murderer was, then they planted the gun on them. They claim they set up an investigation, but they had already decided that the editors were guilty.</p>
<p>Below, we systematically go through their claims and show that there is nothing to them.</p>
<h3 id="s6-3-1">6.3.1 “Bad-Faith” Editors (pg 6)</h3>
<p>The core of the ADL report is based on a collection of 30 supposed “bad-faith” editors. However, the process of selection of this group is a core flaw which undermines the entire report.</p>
<p>To identify “bad-faith editors”, the ADL began by selecting editors, and then searched for reasons to support their selection.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“We identified the group of 30 editors that appear to be in close coordination by calculating the minimum time between edits made on pages related to Israel, Jews, or the Palestinians, such as the 1948 Palestine War and Israeli War Crimes articles. We then analyzed all pages on which they commented or were mentioned, including backend discussion (“talk”) pages and user talk pages (discussion pages for individual editors).”</p>
</blockquote>
<p>To rephrase this, they collected editors that contributed the most to some pages on the topic of Israel/Palestine, and analyzed their contributions and discussions to see whether they liked it. They then declared editors they did not like to be “bad-faith”.</p>
<p>In fact, it shows no such thing. They have no way of determining the editors are acting in “bad faith”—that is simply name-calling for editors who are contributing facts to Wikipedia that the ADL does not like.</p>
<p>To be clear, there is nothing “bad faith” about documenting facts that do not portray Israel in a good light. Though the pro-Israel movement has been keen to call anyone saying anything bad about Israel as antisemitic or anti-Israel, the truth is that Israel has a long history of Crimes Against Humanity, such as ethnic cleansing, apartheid, and genocide. Documenting these in an encyclopedia is not in “bad faith”, it is in fact one of the major purposes for which encyclopedias exist.</p>
<p>Calling these editors “bad faith” is a serious misrepresentation, one that invalidates their entire report. The ADL has demonstrated that their “bad-faith” editors did nothing to be marked as “bad-faith” except contribute to Wikipedia in a way that the ADL does not like.</p>
<p>The ADL did not at any point in the report present any evidence of “bad-faith” behaviour by these editors to label them “bad-faith”, did not demonstrate these editors making false edits, introducing false claims, or of violating Wikipedia policies or rules. In fact, the most evidence they provided, in the section “Manipulation and Disinformation” was prioritizing different content from the ADL on the Wikipedia page for “Hamas”, which as we show in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6#s6-3-4-5">Section 6.3.4.5</a> was spurious.</p>
<h3 id="s6-3-2">6.3.2 “Coordinated Editors” (pg 6-14)</h3>
<p>The ADL’s section on “coordinated editors” attempts to show evidence that their editors are in coordination. They offer no actual evidence of coordination, but instead attempt to infer it by analyzing some shared attributes of the editors.</p>
<p>This is a cardinal sin of data analysis: attempting to find non-standard attributes of a group, and then declaring these attributes as proof of your pre-chosen thesis. This is a massive flaw in their research, one which invalidates the entire thesis of the report.</p>
<p>The report goes through a number of arbitrary attributes where they show that their chosen group is different from other groups they have compiled. They claim that because those editors—on average as a group—score differently in some metrics, that this is proof that the editors are coordinating.</p>
<p>The report claims that these various attributes are evidence of “coordinated” editing, such as: editing more frequently than others, an increase of edits since October 7th, 2023, increased messaging on public “talk” pages, and something they refer to as “tandem editing”.</p>
<p>However, it does not explain why these are evidence of coordination, nor why any of these things are inappropriate.</p>
<h4 id="s6-3-2-1">6.3.2.1 Editing Frequency (pg 6-9)</h4>
<p>One of the main attributes that the ADL attempts to claim is frequency of editing.</p>
<figure class="report-figure"><img src="/images/wikipedia-report/280efb6225e7.webp" alt="" width="1244" height="1004" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<figure class="report-figure"><img src="/images/wikipedia-report/7a2c00b9cced.webp" alt="" width="1260" height="1182" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>In the ADL’s Charts 1 and 2, they show total edits from different groups, demonstrating that their “bad-faith editors” edit more than other groups of editors they have compiled. They do not attempt to explain how this shows collusion, or is otherwise suspicious.</p>
<p>In addition, the two charts use different groupings, with Chart 1 being all 30 top editors, while Chart 2 uses only the top 5 of each group. These cherry-picked datasets suggest that the ADL is choosing to show the data that supports their narrative.</p>
<figure class="report-figure"><img src="/images/wikipedia-report/9a9aa8178fa0.webp" alt="" width="788" height="566" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Chart 3 shows that their chosen editors increased their edits since October 7, 2023.</p>
<p>Again, the ADL does not explain how this contributes to collusion of “bad-faith” editing. In fact, the sole text which accompanies this graphic is “Additionally, this high degree of activity among the bad-faith editors increased substantially following the October 7, 2023, attack on Israel”.</p>
<p>In fact, the evidence shows the behavior of their target group changed in the exact same way as non-“bad-faith” editors of the “Israel-Hamas war” page. It seems that after Israel’s invasion of Gaza in October 2023, Wikipedia editors of all stripes increased their edits on the topic of Palestine/Israel. This seems a natural and obvious phenomenon, that topics in the news and in which a lot is happening, would get more attention.</p>
<h4 id="s6-3-2-2">6.3.2.2 Group Communication (pg 9-10)</h4>
<p>In Chart 4, the ADL claims that their “bad-faith” editors mention each other frequently on Wikipedia’s “Talk pages”.</p>
<figure class="report-figure"><img src="/images/wikipedia-report/9b8bf4855891.webp" alt="" width="778" height="696" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Again, it is unclear what exactly is being claimed here, since the ADL does not spell it out. Their sole statement to explain their point is “This frequency suggests these editors were in much closer communication than is typical on other Wikipedia pages, even those who work on similar content”.</p>
<p>This discrepancy can be explained extremely simply: the ADL chose to group these editors because they appeared together in the same pages as each other. That is, the ADL chose to group these editors for this attribute. They cannot appear shocked when the group chosen for this attribute then demonstrates this attribute.</p>
<p>This is purely circular logic: they frequently participate in the Talk pages of the same Wikipedia pages because they were grouped together by the ADL on that basis.</p>
<p>In addition, there is a relatively simple explanation for why these editors speak more in Talk pages. The Palestine/Israel Articles section of Wikipedia is highly scrutinized, and has strict editing rules. Tagging other editors in Talk pages is a core part of reaching the scholarly consensus which is core to Wikipedia. A significant amount of conversation in Talk pages demonstrates editors serious about their craft, following guidelines and working towards scholarly consensus.</p>
<p>Unfortunately, yet again, the ADL provides no discussion of why we should find this behaviour suspicious.</p>
<p>It also provides evidence against the ADL’s unproven claim of illicit off-platform coordination, since the editors are shown to be more than willing to communicate on Wikipedia.</p>

<p>This is yet another piece of circular logic from the ADL. The ADL grouped these editors for frequently editing similar sets of pages—by definition they will be editing these pages frequently.</p>
<p>Similarly, if two editors are editing the same pages frequently, their edits will occur closer in time than editors who do not edit the same pages frequently.</p>
<p>Indeed, the ADL itself admits as much, stating,</p>

<p>They admit that groups with overlap in the pages they edit, such as the Israel-Hamas war page, also show signs of what they call “tandem editing”. Meanwhile, groups whose pages do not overlap, such as the general editors, do not. This demonstrates that “tandem editing” simply correlates to editing overlapping pages, which is obvious on reflection.</p>
<p>It seems natural, in fact, that editors who work on pages with frequent changes are more likely to continue editing the page further as information develops. Wikipedia also sends notifications to editors when edits are made to pages they follow, which encourages them to return to those pages and make further contributions.</p>
<p>We note at this point that the phrase “tandem editor” is not any sort of term of art. In fact, the term has almost never been used before, with only two casual uses we could find on Wikipedia, dating to 2009 and 2016, in which they were not defined.</p>
<p>Between the undefined, almost-never used term of “tandem editing”, and the fact that this behaviour appears frequently across other groups, it appears that the ADL found nothing at all, and tried to dress up their lack of findings with fancy-sounding words.</p>
<p>The report further claims that “tandem editing” “raises the likelihood that these edits are coordinated off Wikipedia, potentially on sites like Discord”. However, they do not present any actual evidence of editor coordination outside of Wikipedia, and offer no connection between their metric and actual off-site coordination. Moreover, coordinating off of Wikipedia is hardly necessary, given the ADL has shown these users are already coordinating on Wikipedia’s “Talk pages”.</p>
<h4 id="s6-3-2-4">6.3.2.4 Time Between Edits (pg 13-14)</h4>
<p>The report suggests that time between edits is a further reason to consider these editors as suspicious. However, it has already been established that the ADL grouped these editors together based on time between edits, thus it is no surprise that the group displays this property. It is also known that these editors are frequent editors, and naturally, editors who edit more frequently have less time between edits. Thus, this property is not suspicious, but came out of which editors the ADL chose for their “bad-faith” group.</p>
<h4 id="s6-3-2-5">6.3.2.5 “Coordinated editors” summary and verdict</h4>
<p>On pages 6-14, the ADL attempted to show coordination between the editors of their chosen group, concluding “Taken together, this high degree of communication and tandem editing suggest that this group of editors is working together, likely off-site and potentially in violation of Wikipedia’s policies”.</p>
<p>In fact, their data shows no evidence at all. The report merely pulled together a collection of data in which:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>it was not explained why this is suspicious (“editing frequency”, “tandem editing”)</p></li>
<li><p>the data was clearly being manipulated or spun (“editing frequency”, “tandem editing”)</p></li>
<li><p>the data can obviously and easily be explained (increase in edits after October 7, 2023; “time between edits”)</p></li>
<li><p>circular logic (“group communication”, “tandem editing”, “time between edits”)</p></li>
<li><p>the ADL’s explanation is refuted by the data they provide (“tandem editing”, increase in edits after October 7, 2023)</p></li>
</ul>
<p>The evidence is largely spun, unexplained, circular, and embarrassingly shallow, and does not come close to proving anything.</p>
<p>We contend that instead of showing proof, the ADL compiled unconvincing data into a fake scientific veneer, using terms such as “tandem editing” and “time between edits” to try and establish credibility despite a complete lack of evidence.</p>
<h3 id="s6-3-3">6.3.3 Harassment and Intimidation (pg 14-15)</h3>
<p>The report highlights a situation where six Wikipedia editors were banned for harassment by ArbCom, Wikipedia’s court. Here, the ADL omits key information, and claims,</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“Along with direct attacks and bullying, these bad-faith editors used frequent reporting as a means to harass and intimidate. One editor made 37% of their total edits to Wikipedia admin pages (over 13,000 edits in total) , more than 5,000 of which were reports against other editors on administrative notice boards. This high volume of reports suggests these editors focused more on attacking those they viewed as opponents rather than contributing to articles. Such harassment can be a form of area denial, making it impossible for good-faith editors to participate, and can lead to editors participating less or leaving altogether.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-395" id="fnref-395">395</a></sup></p>
</blockquote>
<p>The ADL omits that this case was built around evidence provided by a pro-Israel editor, who was also banned for abusing the system. As we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-5">Section 4.5</a>, the editor started 18 cases against perceived ideological enemies. Two pro-Israel editors were also removed for abuse in that case, and over 15 editors were removed in total in 2025.</p>
<p>Furthermore, this case actually disproves a key ADL complaint, that being that Wikipedia is not taking sufficient action on issues emerging within sections that contain articles related to Palestine-Israel. This arbitration proves that Wikipedia does in fact take allegations seriously, and they have an extensive process for handling issues.</p>
<p>If anything, it seems that Israel’s resources are being applied to manipulate Wikipedia—the exact complaint the ADL is making about editors who they do not agree with.</p>

<h4 id="s6-3-4-6">6.3.4.6 “Manipulation and Disinformation” summary and verdict</h4>
<p>On pages 15-24 of their report, the ADL attempted to show that the editors it chose took part in coordinated, deceptive, or ideologically-motivated editing. The evidence presented focused on patterns of editing, such as editing outside of Palestine-Israel Articles, making many small edits, how they spent their time, as well as examples focussing on specific content.</p>
<p>In fact, the patterns were spurious and failed to show any suspicious behaviour:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>They framed ordinary and best-practice Wikipedia activity as sinister (“avoiding detection”, “making minor edits”, “a million edits”) when it in fact demonstrates good-faith editing by those editors.</p></li>
<li><p>They suggested that Wikipedia editors were being paid, without actual evidence, using a single cherry-picked example which actually demonstrated the opposite (“time spent editing”).</p></li>
<li><p>They significantly exaggerated the total impact of these editors, when in fact their total edit count puts them outside the top 5000 editors, and where the 20 most prolific Wikipedia editors each have more edits than all of the grouped editors combined.</p></li>
</ul>
<p>The examples were worse:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>The ADL hid the context from the examples, when that context demonstrates that they are simply part of a collaborative Wikipedia editing process (“Palestinian violence”)</p></li>
<li><p>They ignored standard Wikipedia policies such as “undue weight” (WP:UNDUE, in “Hamas edits” and “Swastika Kite”) and the MOS:CONTENTIOUS and MOS:LEAD policies (in “Hamas edits”), instead choosing to ascribe malicious intent to the editors.</p></li>
<li><p>They make unsupported claims of antisemitism because they did not like content that is supported by 17 separate scholarly references (“Zionism page”)</p></li>
<li><p>They selectively edited a quote to make editors seem antisemitic, when the full quote shows no such thing (“1948 Palestine War page”)</p></li>
</ul>
<p>According to the ADL, the editors in question made around 1,000,000 edits to Wikipedia, an ample corpus in which the ADL should have been able to find a large range of biased editing. Given their best efforts to provide evidence relied instead on cutting context and selective editing, we are left to conclude that the ADL could not find valid or persuasive evidence.</p>
<p>In addition, in almost every case, the ADL provided no links to the Wikipedia edits that could be used to investigate and replicate their data. Given that Wikipedia provides clear permalinks to every edit, we believe the ADL chose not to include links to their evidence because it was so low quality, and the raw evidence would undermine their claims.</p>
<h3 id="s6-3-5">6.3.5 Voting Patterns (pg 24-29)</h3>
<p>The ADL presents overlap in the voting patterns of their chosen editors as signs of collusion. They present 19 articles that editors largely voted to keep, and 18 articles that the editors largely voted to delete, out of a larger corpus.</p>

<h2 id="s6-4">6.4 Detailed Critique of Case Study 2</h2>
<p>In “Case Study 2: Bias in Arabic-language Wikipedia content on Hamas” in the ADL’s report,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-441" id="fnref-441">441</a></sup> the ADL focuses on analyzing specific Arabic-language Wikipedia pages, claiming that Arabic Wikipedia contains significant pro-Hamas bias, perpetuates pro-Hamas propaganda, and “flout[s]” Wikipedia’s rules for neutrality.</p>
<p>However, in our analysis of the sections they cite, we find that the ADL frequently sees pro-Hamas bias where none exists. Even though the ADL’s examples are cherry-picked from over 1.2M articles on Wikipedia, nearly all of the bias the ADL alleged turned out to be either neutral facts about Hamas, viewed through the organization’s own biases, or passages the ADL misunderstood due to misinterpretation of sections translated from Arabic.</p>
<p>Instead of analyzing the original Arabic language articles directly, they examined English translations, which resulted in the ADL inferring meaning that was not present in the original. Often, this analysis hinged on interpreting a single English word, such as “garrison” or “unique”, to infer violence or praise.</p>
<p>The ADL’s analysis shows an apparent failure to grasp the full meaning of the original Arabic text. By relying on an English translation rather than examining the terms within their proper linguistic, cultural, and/or Islamic contexts, the organization reached several incorrect conclusions. This includes an overly simplistic and decontextualized interpretation of a passage from a Hadith, a significant collection of Islamic texts.</p>
<p>To further build its case, the ADL singled out more minor and less significant pages to bolster its argument. Given the vast range of articles in Arabic Wikipedia, it is unreasonable to expect every single article to be completely free of bias. The same is true on English Wikipedia, where articles with less traffic, fewer edits, and fewer contributors naturally have fewer chances to improve their quality.</p>
<p>The ADL also claims that the specific passages they chose to analyze contain fewer citations or broken citations. This is a common problem across Wikipedia, and it does not demonstrate that the content is biased, that it endorses Hamas, or that it constitutes propaganda. These are separate claims and require separate evidence.</p>
<p>A main flaw is the ADL’s assumption that Arabic Wikipedia should be an exact copy of its English counterpart. Rather, Arabic Wikipedia serves 450M people spread across 25 countries with diverse governments, cultures, dialects, and religions, and is not supposed to be identical to English Wikipedia. While not all Arabs are Muslim, Islam carries particularly significant influence across the Arabic-speaking world.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-442" id="fnref-442">442</a></sup></p>
<p>The ADL’s analysis relies on stripping this context from the articles under evaluation. In many cases, it appears that the ADL even lacks the understanding of the context, such as in the section that cites a Hadith. Several of their analyses rely on important words such as <em>liberation</em>, <em>resistance</em>, <em>martyr</em>, and <em>jihad</em>. It seems that the ADL either lacks a grasp of the full nuance and context of these Arabic words, or assumes its audience not to care.</p>
<p>Arabic Wikipedia is geared towards Arabic speakers, but the ADL, an American organization, interprets its content through a lens that is not only biased, but that also ignores the linguistic, cultural, and/or religious context needed to adequately analyze it. This results in a severely flawed analysis, and in several cases, the ADL appears to willfully misconstrue certain sections. This allows the ADL to slander editors with the term “pro-Hamas” without merit.</p>
<h3 id="s6-4-1">6.4.1 Hamas Examples</h3>
<p>We analyze the ADL’s examples concerning Hamas, each of which, the ADL claims, “glorifies the terrorist organization, minimizes its atrocities, and, in places, appears to function as propaganda that does not abide by Wikipedia’s NPOV requirements or its content source standards”. We found that none of the ADL’s examples held up.</p>
<h4 id="s6-4-1-1">6.4.1.1 Pro-Palestinian Phrases for Liberation Framed as Praise for Hamas</h4>
<p>The ADL claims that neutral, simple descriptions of Hamas are glorification of the organization. Two examples are the phrase “from the river to the sea” and (quoting the ADL) ‘“liberation” of “Palestine”’.</p>
<p>It is absurd to say that describing Hamas’s goal as the liberation of Palestine glorifies Hamas. It is a simple description of the goal of the organization. Many other organizations have the same goal, though prefer a different means.</p>
<p>Similarly, “from the river to the sea” is a common phrase associated with Palestinian liberation, referring to the land between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea—the area from which Israel ethnically cleansed Palestinians and where they seek self-determination. In this particular case, it is a direct quote that describes their goals, provided via a reference to the Wilson Center, an organization funded by the U.S. Congress to provide a “trusted, fiercely nonpartisan bridge between scholarship and public policy”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-443" id="fnref-443">443</a></sup></p>
<p>The ADL makes no attempt to explain their problem with these phrases.<br />
<br />
Notably, the ADL even puts quotation marks around “Palestine”, signaling an extreme stance that questions Palestine’s very existence. This choice demonstrates not only how badly the ADL misunderstands the context of Arabic Wikipedia, but also how far its views stray from the rational mainstream.</p>
<figure class="report-figure"><img src="/images/wikipedia-report/59ac589aa9fc.webp" alt="" width="964" height="869" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<h4 id="s6-4-1-2">6.4.1.2 “A Vision of the Liberation of Palestine” and Translating Religious Content</h4>
<p>A clear example of the ADL’s lack of understanding of the Arabic text occurs in their discussion of the section “A vision of the liberation of Palestine”. This section contains religious references, which are not accurately translated, especially considering the religious context, further showing the ADL’s lack of contextual understanding.</p>
<p>The ADL takes specific issue with the line “a day’s garrison in the cause of God is better than the world and everything in it”, claiming that it “bolster[s] Hamas’s positions, rather than providing neutral, noteworthy information”, and that it “advocat[es] Hamas’s violent worldview”.</p>
<p>In fact, this is a quote from a Hadith, a reported saying of the Prophet Muhammad, which the Wikipedia page notes as being narrated by Hadith scholars Al-Bukhari, Muslim, Al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah.</p>
<figure class="report-figure"><img src="/images/wikipedia-report/c70aeb705583.webp" alt="" width="1286" height="514" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>In fact, the English translation of the Arabic word <em>ribat</em> as “garrison” is flawed. <em>Ribat</em> carries specific meaning within the Hadith, and the linguistic, historical, and religious context appears unknown to the ADL.</p>
<p><em>Ribat</em> can be understood as <strong>“</strong>the act of standing guard for the sake of Allah” and “holds profound significance in Islamic tradition”. Historically, <em>ribat</em> referred to protecting vulnerable frontiers from enemy attacks, although now can be understood as: “standing vigil for the sake of Allah, guarding community vulnerabilities…its essence may extend to various forms of service and sacrifice in the way of Allah”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-444" id="fnref-444">444</a></sup></p>
<p>The English term “garrison” more commonly refers to troops stationed to guard a specific location. By understanding <em>ribat</em> as “garrison”, a literal translation, the ADL emphasizes a warlike or combative interpretation, which ignores the full meaning. The ADL’s use of this translation misses the specific religious context, and shows how ADL lacks the necessary linguistic, historical, and religious context in its analysis.</p>
<p>Contextual religious translations of the Hadith do not use “garrison”. The standard translation on <a href="http://sunnah.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">Sunnah.com</a>, from Dr. Muhammad Muhsin Khan, translates it as “To guard Muslims from infidels in Allah's Cause for one day is better than the world and whatever is on its surface…”,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-445" id="fnref-445">445</a></sup> while <em>A Manual of Hadith</em> translates it to “A day’s frontier guard in the way of Allah is better than the world and what is in it”,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-446" id="fnref-446">446</a></sup> and <em>Sahih Muslim</em> as "Observing Ribat (guarding the Muslim frontiers in the cause of Allah) for a day and a night is better than fasting and standing for night prayer for a whole month".<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-447" id="fnref-447">447</a></sup></p>
<p>The ADL’s choice of a simple context-free translation leads to its error. Many English translations of this hadith exist, which the ADL could have used to better understand the words in context, had they understood what they were looking at.</p>
<p>By relying on an English translation without linguistic, historical, or religious context, the ADL mischaracterizes the passage and fails to establish their claim of glorification or praise of Hamas.</p>

The ADL introduces a connection of tunnels and the October 7 attack, and then uses that to claim the section “lauds the violent actions of the terrorist organization”.</p>
<p>The actual Wikipedia subsection describes a construction project. It does not mention October 7 in any way. The ADL introduces a new concept, and then attributes it to the Wikipedia editors in an attempt to insert violence which does not exist in the actual text.</p>

<h4 id="s6-4-1-5">6.4.1.5 “Women’s Work”</h4>
<p>As we discuss in this section, it is worth remembering that Hamas refers not just to the Al-Qassam brigades, Hamas’s military wing, but to a broader organization encompassing political, military, and social activities. Hamas also governed Gaza, with responsibility for overseeing civilian services such as healthcare, education, and public administration.</p>
<p>The ADL’s major issue in this section rests on the use of “the first-person plural”, in the phrase “we find them as mothers”. Though they did not state the objection, we expect it might be either that the ADL believes this introduces the authors’ opinion, or that it is written in Wikipedia’s voice.</p>
<p>However, this is a conventional rhetorical construction in Arabic. The grammatical use of “we” does not itself demonstrate that Wikipedia’s editors personally endorse the proposition that follows, much less that they “advocate for jihad”, as the ADL claims.</p>
<p>In English, this might be written more simply as “they are mothers”. The ADL is assigning meaning to an ordinary grammatical construction without evidence.</p>
<p>The ADL also criticizes the sentence for referring to raising children “on the love of God and jihad for His sake”. Though they do not state it, we can infer that the ADL believes that “jihad” is necessarily violent, which is how the term is commonly used in English.</p>
<p>However, the page is not written in English, and the Arabic use of the word jihad is a more complex term. According to Wikipedia, “In an Islamic context, [jihad] encompasses almost any effort to make personal and social life conform with God's guidance, such as an internal struggle against evil in oneself, efforts to build a good Muslim community (ummah), and struggle to defend Islam.”<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-449" id="fnref-449">449</a></sup> This is supported by scholarship on Islam.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-450" id="fnref-450">450</a></sup></p>
<p>Further, the actual wording does not “advocate” for jihad, as the ADL claims—the actual wording is descriptive.</p>
<p>The ADL also criticizes the page for referring to suicide bombers as “martyrs”. In this section in particular, the ADL expects Arabic Wikipedia to be written on the basis of its worldview, in which occupation is good and resistance to it is bad. In Arabic, martyr is a common term to refer to people understood to have died in or for the Palestinian struggle, including civilians as well as combatants. Simply translating the word as “martyr” and then treating it as evidence of pro-Hamas propaganda ignores the substantially different cultural and political meaning the term carries in Arabic.</p>

<p>In this section, the ADL cherry-picks pages in the search for “biased” or “unreliable” content “related to Hamas”. Arabic Wikipedia has over a million pages, and over a hundred of them are directly related to Hamas, with presumably many more tangentially related.</p>

<h4 id="s6-4-2-1">6.4.2.1 The Shalit Deal and “Praise” for Hamas</h4>
<h4 id="s6-4-2-1-1">6.4.2.1.1 “Glorification” for the Gilad Shalit Deal</h4>
<p>The ADL claims that Wikipedia “praises” Hamas on its page for the Shalit Deal, referring to the 2011 prisoner exchange with Gilad Shalit. <p>Furthermore, LLMs are already biased against Palestinians and prioritizing pro-Israel narratives, so attempts to use AI and other centralized approaches for “at scale” abuse or bias prevention will be faulty. <p>Ultimately, then, the issue seems to be that the ADL does not like the experts used by Wikipedia already—historians, scholars, and noted experts in their respective fields—and wishes to replace them with “experts” whose perspectives are friendlier to Israel, and align more closely with their own.</p>
<p>Most importantly though, the tools Wikipedia already has for preventing bias and other issues “at scale” have proven to be effective. Despite the ADL’s claims, Wikipedia remains very neutral. And it is in fact this which the ADL is pushing back on—almost all other media out there is biased towards Israel, and its objection is that Wikipedia is not.</p>
<h4 id="s6-7-1-2">6.7.1.2 Recommendations for AI Companies</h4>
<p>The ADL recommends removing Wikipedia as source material in Large Language Models (LLMs), replacing it with other sources, or warning users that it is not reliable.</p>
<p>Again, the ADL has failed to demonstrate an actual problem with Wikipedia. Its claims about the bias within Wikipedia are false and absurd, as discussed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-6">Section 6</a>, and even sometimes intentionally taken out of context to present their narrative. The ADL has falsified this narrative in an effort to damage LLMs by making them biased towards Israel and away from human rights.</p>
<p>By attempting to manipulate LLMs in this way, they attempt to erase important historical information pertaining to the region, from even prior to the establishment of the Israeli state. Such manipulation obscures the history of Israel, and its efforts to colonize, occupy, and settle Palestine.</p>
<p>They recommend that LLMs instead rely on “other relevant and trustworthy sources, like Reuters”. But, a significant amount of mainstream media is much less trustworthy and more biased than Wikipedia. In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-2#s2-3-4">Section 2.3.4</a>, we discuss multiple reports which analyze media bias in headlines and content, across the mainstream media landscape.</p>
<p>Wikipedia’s high quality comes from its significant use of academic papers and scholarly books, which operate at a far higher standard than news media. Wikipedia’s approach allows editors to find and remove unvalidated information, to ensure that each fact is thoroughly cited and reputable, and provides many eyes to ensure high quality. Despite this, the ADL seeks to replace this incredible resource with a reliance on already biased news media, over which it has significantly more influence.</p>
<p>Similarly, it advocates for search companies to downgrade Wikipedia content, not because it isn’t trustworthy and factual, but because it is, and because the trustworthy facts are inconvenient to their narrative.</p>
<h4 id="s6-7-1-3">6.7.1.3 Recommendations for Government</h4>
<p>The ADL asks governments to step in and take steps that would be disastrous for civil rights on the basis of false claims of “antisemitism”. As discussed previously, the ADL did not make any effort to actually present evidence of antisemitism on English Wikipedia.</p>
<p>Despite this, the ADL tries to bring in the government to, among other things, “institut[e] a private identity verification program for editors”. This suggestion would require Wikipedia editors to register in order to be able to edit Wikipedia. This is a chilling effect, which would harm normal Wikipedia editors, but would not harm pro-Israel editors, who are aligned with the government in this case.</p>
<p>We are once again reminded that the ADL is not a civil rights organization, but one who will reject civil rights if it helps them achieve their goal of promoting pro-Israel narratives, which is made possible through obscuring Israel’s crimes.</p>
<p>We note that one of the ADL’s suggestions has already been established, “holding hearings on the topic of bias and antisemitism on Wikipedia”, as discussed previously.</p>
<p>By asking the government to step in to address fake claims, the ADL attempts to introduce new ways to influence Wikipedia content. The U.S. government has been systematically bribed by AIPAC to be pro-Israel, whether through funding campaigns to allow pro-Israel candidates to join the government, or to suppress human rights- based decision-making, which would limit military support the U.S. provides to Israel. These AIPAC-aligned politicians are now the ones that the ADL is asking to regulate Wikipedia.</p>
