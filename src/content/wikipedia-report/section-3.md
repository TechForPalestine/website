<p>Israel—and its extensive propaganda movement—has, over the last twenty years, made at least eighteen publicly known efforts to directly manipulate Wikipedia. In this section, we discuss past campaigns and efforts that have attempted to edit Wikipedia to be more aligned with Israeli narratives.</p>
<p>These eighteen different efforts, which we have identified through public reports, and at least nine of which are ongoing, are coordinated efforts to influence the direction of Wikipedia. These campaigns have been operating openly, even promoting their efforts via mainstream media. We discuss each of the known attempts, bringing this collection together for the first time.</p>
<p>In this section, we only discuss <em>direct manipulation</em> of Wikipedia, where editors work to change the content or to remove editors. Influence attacks and organizations monitoring Wikipedia editors are discussed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4">Section 4</a>.</p>

<h2 id="s3-1">3.1 JIDF (2001-2015)</h2>
<p>The JIDF was an extremist “hacktivist” group, which operated in secret beginning in 2000, and then publicly between 2008 and 2015.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-69" id="fnref-69">69</a></sup> It described itself as a “collective of activists and a non-violent protest group with over 5,000 members and supporters, which seeks in its own way to counter anti-Semitic content [that] promotes terrorism online in places including [...] Wikipedia”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-70" id="fnref-70">70</a></sup></p>
<p>This extremist group shared their views on Palestinians in an interview, in which they stated, “Palestinians should be transferred out of Israeli territories. They can live in any of the other many Arab states. We are against all land concessions to our enemies. We are against the release of terrorist prisoners from Israeli prisons”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-71" id="fnref-71">71</a></sup></p>
<p>According to Haaretz, “JIDF's measures ‘include reporting Wikipedia editors it claims are anti-Israel, and taking action against entries seen as including one-sided or false accounts of the history of Israel and the Mideast conflict’”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-72" id="fnref-72">72</a></sup></p>
<h2 id="s3-2">3.2 Israeli Government (2007)</h2>
<p>The first documented case of Israel’s manipulation of Wikipedia stems from 2007, when a wide-scale probe of the IP addresses associated with editors were traced back to their sources.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-73" id="fnref-73">73</a></sup> As a result, “A computer linked to the Israeli government twice tried to delete an entire article about the West Bank wall that was critical of the policy. An edit from the same address also modified the entry for Hezbollah describing all its operations as being ‘mostly military in nature’”.</p>
<h2 id="s3-3">3.3 CAMERA (2008)</h2>
<p>The Committee for Accuracy on Middle East Reporting in America (CAMERA), an organization predicated on attacking any reporting perceived to hold anti-Israel bias, was revealed to be manipulating Wikipedia in a story first broken on April 21, 2008 by the Electronic Intifada (EI).<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-74" id="fnref-74">74</a></sup></p>
<p>They revealed that CAMERA was undertaking a secretive and coordinated campaign to recruit editors to infiltrate Wikipedia to manipulate topics relating to Israel, after the outlet obtained a series of emails sent by CAMERA senior research analyst Gilead Ini and others in which their plans were exposed.</p>
<p>These documents explain that CAMERA carefully encouraged the editors it recruited to disguise their activity by first editing other topics, selecting an innocuous username, and weaponizing Wikipedia’s own policies when making edits, all while seeding anti-Palestinian racism.</p>
<p>In an email on March 17th, Gilead Ini shares specific advice on how pro-Israel propaganda or opinion might be passed off as fact in a way that meets Wikipedia’s strict guidelines. The email reads:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“So, for example, imagine that you get of or modify a problematic sentence in an article alleging that ‘Palestinian [sic] become suicide bombers to respond to Israel’s oppressive policies.’ You should, in parallel, leave a comment on that article’s discussion page (either after or before making the change). Avoid defending the edit by arguing that ‘Israel’s policies aren’t ‘oppression,’ they are defensive. And anyway Palestinians obviously become suicide bombers for other reasons for example hate education!’ Instead, describe how this sentence violates Wikipedia’s policies and guidelines. One of the core principles is that assertions should adhere to a Neutral Point of View, usually abbreviated NPOV. (The opposite of NPOV is POV, or Point of View, which is basically another way of saying subjective statement, or opinion.) So it would be best to note on the discussion page that “This sentence violates Wikipedia’s NPOV policy, since the description of Israel’s policies are ‘oppressive’ is an opinion. In addition, it is often noted by Middle East experts that one of the reasons Palestinians decide to become suicide bombers is hate education and glorification of martyrdom in Palestinian society…’”</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The campaign’s long-term goal was to enlist editors who would avoid topics related to Palestine and Israel altogether, instead building up credibility by editing other topics until they could be elected as administrators, at which time they would be able to exert greater influence on editing other topics to support Zionist ideology.</p>
<p>Another email from Ini, this time quoted in a May 2008 article from The American Prospect, demonstrates the intent to keep the operation secret.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-75" id="fnref-75">75</a></sup> The email reads, “Please forward this alert to people committed to fair and factual reporting about Israel and the Middle East…Do not forward it to members of the news media”.</p>
<p>Wikipedia’s administrators quickly responded to the news of CAMERA’s campaign by banning Zeq—an editor who had worked closely with the pro-Israel group to develop their tactics to subvert the online encyclopedia’s rules—from editing any pages related to Palestine and Israel. Ini shut down the off-Wiki group chat shortly thereafter, explicitly citing the leak to the Electronic Intifada.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-76" id="fnref-76">76</a></sup></p>
<h2 id="s3-4">3.4 Yesha Council (2010)</h2>
<p>In 2010, a series of courses on becoming a Wikipedia editor were launched by the Yesha Council and Israel Sheli (“My Israel”), two organizations deeply entrenched in the Israeli settler takeover of the West Bank.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-77" id="fnref-77">77</a></sup> In a report from Israel National News, Naftali Bennett—who was director of the Yesha Council at the time and later became the Prime Minister of Israel—made the purpose of these courses explicitly clear.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-78" id="fnref-78">78</a></sup> He says,</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“The goal of the day is to teach people how to edit in Wikipedia, which is the number 1 source of information in the world. As way of example, if someone searches the Gaza flotilla,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-79" id="fnref-79">79</a></sup> we wanna be there. We wanna be the guys who influence what is written there, how it’s written, and to ensure that it’s balanced and Zionist in nature.”</p>
</blockquote>
<figure class="report-figure"><img src="/images/wikipedia-report/acc96dee5531.webp" alt="" width="999" height="687" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Prospective editors who attended these classes would have an opportunity to contribute to Israel’s narrative. According to a report from Haaretz published on August 18, 2010, “The Yesha Council also announced a prize for the ‘Best Zionist Editor’—the person who over the next four years incorporates the most ‘Zionist’ changes in the encyclopedia. That lucky encyclopedist would receive a trip in a hot-air balloon over Israel”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-80" id="fnref-80">80</a></sup></p>
<p>This implies the Yesha Council was intending to recruit editors into long-term volunteer careers as defenders of Zionism on Wikipedia, in direct violation of multiple Wikipedia policies.</p>
<p>While this campaign to manipulate the open-source encyclopedia received more attention publicly, it appears that it sparked very little direct action from Wikipedia. A separate campaign by the Yesha council to manipulate Wikipedia was discovered in 2024 and is detailed in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-3#s3-14">Section 3.14</a>.</p>
<h2 id="s3-5">3.5 Kohelet Policy Forum (2013–ongoing)</h2>
<p>In July 2023, when Israel was in the midst of widespread civil unrest due to attempts to overhaul the judiciary, a Haaretz report revealed that Israel’s Wikipedia manipulations had continued.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-81" id="fnref-81">81</a></sup> It claimed that the Kohelet Policy Forum, a right-wing, U.S.-funded Israeli think tank,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-82" id="fnref-82">82</a></sup> “has long employed writers to edit Wikipedia entries on its behalf” on Hebrew Wikipedia, going back to 2013.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-83" id="fnref-83">83</a></sup></p>
<p>While the report is careful to state that these editors disclose their activity in accordance with the platform’s policies, editors were presumably paid to manipulate Wikipedia to conform to Kohelet’s extreme right-wing views.</p>
<p>Haaretz also reveals that one of those paid editors was creating sockpuppet accounts to unduly influence consensus decisions, a flagrant violation of Wikipedia’s rules. These sockpuppets were created in 2023, showing over a decade of manipulating Wikipedia, and indicating this behaviour likely continues.</p>

<h2 id="s3-8">3.8 The Israel Group (2015*–2021)</h2>
<p>The Israel Group is a U.S.-based non-profit founded by a former Israeli soldier. They announced in 2019 a program called Wiki-Israel, which it had been working on “for many years” (we conservatively used 2015 in our timeline). The program’s intention was to dox what it referred to as “anti-Israel” editors on Wikipedia, following up on this claim by doxing two Wikipedia editors in 2020.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-90" id="fnref-90">90</a></sup></p>
<p>Speaking to the Washington Free Beacon—later known for alleging plagiarism against then-Harvard President Claudine Gay—the Israel Group claimed that it “has been working for many years, under the radar, on a confidential initiative, Wiki-Israel, that combats Wikipedia's antisemitic bias against Israel”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-91" id="fnref-91">91</a></sup></p>
<p>David Swindle, a former employee of Israel Group, describes spending “20 hours a week” on Wikipedia, and says the group considered lawsuits, and doxxing editors.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-92" id="fnref-92">92</a></sup></p>
<p>The Israel Group website refers to it as “Energizing Zionists”, and contains lists of editors and articles that the Israel Group believes are anti-Israel. The founder of the Israel Group died in 2021,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-93" id="fnref-93">93</a></sup> and it appears the site has been unmaintained since then.</p>

<h2 id="s3-16">3.16 Attacks on editors via ArbCom (2022-2025)</h2>
<p>The widely-reported decision by ArbCom to ban pro-Palestine editors appears to be the results of a well-resourced, multi-pronged attack by pro-Israel Wikipedia editors and journalists to manipulate the encyclopedia. Specifically, it sought to remove editors that it perceived as being pro-Palestine, in order to tip the editing balance towards pro-Israel editors.</p>
<p>ArbCom, Wikipedia’s arbitration committee which handles disputes between editors, has increasingly banned editors of Palestine-Israel content, banning at least 14 editors in 2025.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-109" id="fnref-109">109</a></sup> ArbCom is composed of editor-elected volunteers, and are tasked with policing misconduct. Editors bring cases to ArbCom, who then collect and discuss evidence, and vote on appropriate conclusions.</p>
<p>In a widely reported decision, in January 2025, referred to as PIA5, ArbCom banned 8 editors, two believed to be pro-Israel and six believed to be pro-Palestine.</p>
<p>However, what is often left out of this story is the significant pro-Israel resources that went into PIA5, with the goal of allowing pro-Israel editors to increase their control and propaganda on the platform.</p>
<p>One pro-Israel editor, BilledMammal, produced an evidently well-resourced collection of evidence that formed a key element of PIA5. <h3 id="s3-17-1">3.17.1 Wales’ post</h3>
<p>The full content of Wales’ post<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-125" id="fnref-125">125</a></sup> reads:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>“This message is from me, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User:Jimbo_Wales" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">Jimbo Wales</a>, co-founder of Wikipedia.</em></p>
<p>I believe that Wikipedia is at its best when we can have reasonable discussion rooted in a commitment to write articles that reflect a neutral point of view. I believe that's especially important on highly difficult or contentious topics.</p>
<p>I was asked point-blank in a high profile media interview about this article, and I answered with transparency and honesty: this article fails to meet our high standards and needs immediate attention.</p>
<p>As many of you will know, I have been leading an NPOV working group and studying the issue of neutrality in Wikipedia across many articles and topic areas including “Zionism”. While this article is a particularly egregious example, there is much more work to do. It should go without saying that I am writing this in my personal capacity, and I am not speaking on behalf of the Wikimedia Foundation or anyone else!</p>
<p>I assume good faith of everyone who has worked on this Gaza “genocide” article. At present, the lede and the overall presentation state, in Wikipedia’s voice, that Israel is committing genocide, although that claim is highly contested. This is a violation of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:NPOV" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:NPOV</a> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:ATTRIBUTEPOV" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:ATTRIBUTEPOV</a> that requires immediate correction. Remember: "This policy is non-negotiable, and the principles upon which it is based cannot be superseded by other policies or guidelines, nor by editor consensus."</p>
<p>A neutral approach would begin with a formulation such as: “Multiple governments, NGOs, and legal bodies have described or rejected the characterization of Israel’s actions in Gaza as genocide.”</p>
<p><strong>What I’m asking for</strong></p>
<p><strong>Be bold and start editing</strong> (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:BOLD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:BOLD</a>). Move from debate to concrete improvements immediately.</p>
<p><strong>Assume good faith</strong> (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:AGF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:AGF</a>). Avoid personalizing disagreements. Focus only on text and sources.</p>
<p><strong>Attribute, don’t assert</strong> (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:ATTRIBUTEPOV" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:ATTRIBUTEPOV</a>). The lead and body must not declare a legal conclusion. Present who says what, with attribution and dates.</p>
<p><strong>Balance by due weight</strong> (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:UNDUE" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:UNDUE</a>). Include significant, high-quality sources from all sides—governments, courts, NGOs, commentators. No side should speak in Wikipedia’s own voice.</p>
<p><strong>Use the best sources</strong> (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:RS" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:RS</a> / <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:V" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:V</a>). Prefer primary official statements and major secondary coverage. Avoid synthesis (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:SYN" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:SYN</a> / <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:NOR" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-describedby="report-new-tab-hint">WP:NOR</a>).</p>
<p><strong>Clarify scope.</strong> Separate factual reporting on conduct and casualties from legal characterization. Editors with strong, policy-compliant sources who have felt sidelined are welcome. Your participation is needed now. Editors are reminded this article falls under the Arab–Israeli conflict topic area; all participation must comply with the corresponding editing restrictions and civility expectations.</p>
<p>By focusing on verifiable sources and neutrality, we can quickly deliver an article that meets our standards for contentious topics.”</p>
<p>- Statement from Jimmy Wales” in Talk:Gaza genocide/Archive 22</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="s3-17-2">3.17.2 Responses</h3>

<h3 id="s3-17-3">3.17.3 Influence</h3>
<p>As well as being founder of Wikipedia, Wales is a member of the Board of Trustees of Wikimedia Foundation, one who admits he holds strong, biased views in support of Israel. For both those reasons, he should not have taken part in that discussion.</p>
<p>At the time of the incident, he was also lead of a working group on the Neutral Point of View, creating a baseline NPOV policy that could be adopted by multiple Wikipedia language groups.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-130" id="fnref-130">130</a></sup></p>
<p>Though Wales claims he was acting in a personal capacity, his actions do not match that. He starts by asserting his authority as “co-founder of Wikipedia”. He continues by adding pressure by highlighting a “high profile media interview” in which he was asked about the article, and he followed by saying the article “needs immediate attention”.</p>
<p>He further cited his authority on the topic by saying ‘I have been leading an NPOV working group and studying the issue of neutrality in Wikipedia across many articles and topic areas including “Zionism”’.</p>
<p>Only then does he say he is writing in his personal capacity.</p>
<p>He goes on to say that the article needs “immediate correction”, and quotes “This policy is non-negotiable”. He then suggests new wording that he would prefer.</p>
<h3 id="s3-17-4">3.17.4 Abuse of Power</h3>
<p>To clarify, this is an abuse of power, leveraged to change content of an important Wikipedia page to take a pro-Israel slant.</p>
<p>One of the most influential people on Wikipedia begins by asserting his influence in multiple ways, then demands specific changes to a page based on his biases, despite significant work by Wikipedia editors to discuss and come to consensus on the topic.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-131" id="fnref-131">131</a></sup> He amplified the importance by citing a “high profile media interview”, and charged the debate by demanding it be changed with high priority.</p>
<p>In addition, the idea that a founder, senior leader, and Board Trustee of an organization can speak without authority is laughable. If Wales had wanted to simply bring up the point, he could have done it anonymously. Instead, he started by asserting who he was, just in case his username was not known to readers.</p>
<p>As a member of the Board of Trustees, his aggressive attempt to change Wikipedia content on the basis of his personal opinion violates the Abuse of Power section of Wikimedia Foundation’s Universal Code of Conduct (section 3.2).<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-132" id="fnref-132">132</a></sup></p>
<p>It further violates the Code of Conduct of the Wikimedia Foundation Board of Trustees, Section 4,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-133" id="fnref-133">133</a></sup> which states:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>The Foundation recognizes as bullying behavior that may be characterized as offensive, intimidating</strong>, malicious or insulting, <strong>or an abuse or misuse of power through means intended to undermine</strong>, humiliate, denigrate or injure the recipient. <strong>Bullying does not need to be deliberate; someone may demonstrate bullying behavior without intending to.</strong> Whichever form it takes, it is unwarranted and unwelcome to the individual and will often cause embarrassment, fear, humiliation or distress to an individual or group of individuals. <strong>Bullying often results from a misuse of individual power derived from status/position</strong>, physical strength or force of personality. It can also arise from collective power arising out of strength of numbers.</p>
<p>- Code of Conduct of the Wikimedia Foundation Board of Trustees, Section 4, “Respect &amp; Fairness” (emphasis ours)</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Indeed, it also contradicts the Wikimedia Foundation’s response to the letter from Ted Cruz which we discuss in <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-4#s4-4">Section 4.4</a>. Cruz asked “what supervision, oversight, or influence, if any, the Wikimedia Foundation has over the editing community”,<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-134" id="fnref-134">134</a></sup> to which the Foundation responded “The Wikimedia Foundation provides technology and legal support but does not write or control the content on Wikipedia—volunteer editors do”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-135" id="fnref-135">135</a></sup></p>
<p>We refer to this as “ongoing”, as Wales has not apologized, backed-down, or indicated that he would not repeat the behaviour. In subsequent interviews, he reiterated his approach, saying “I'm going to make my case as best I can”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-136" id="fnref-136">136</a></sup> When asked how he felt about the editors who had built a consensus and stated “Yeah, I think they’re wrong”.<sup class="fn-ref"><a href="#fn-137" id="fnref-137">137</a></sup></p>
<p>In <a href="/wikipedia-report-2026/section-7#s7-1-6">Section 7.1.6</a>, we recommend Jimmy Wales step down for his abuse of power.</p>
